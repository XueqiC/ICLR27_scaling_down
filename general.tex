\section{Generalization to Unseen Models and Configurations}
\label{sec:general}

Once a form is chosen on the development set, its coefficients are
frozen and it predicts three kinds of unseen object: compression
settings outside the fitted set, model states of new sizes or
pretraining stages, and, across both, the three capabilities.
Table~\ref{tab:pred_main} lists every registered test with the
candidate error and the strongest relevant baseline; Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}
shows where the candidates gain over a source-free reference and where
they do not. Errors are MAE in nats per token; ``interpolation'' and
``extrapolation'' refer to the fitted range of the setting.

\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

\paragraph{Pruning densities.} Inside the fitted density range the
source-conditioned forms predict math and code and do not predict QA.
On the three checkpoints that entered no fit, at densities between the
grid points (0.85, 0.675, 0.575), the delivered power form has MAE
0.243, 0.236, and 0.678 for math, code, and QA: it beats the
strength-only curve (0.450, 0.488, 0.579) and zero change on every
checkpoint, it has the lowest math error of all predictors (A2 is
near-exact on math at 1.4B@112k), it ties the median development
curve on code (0.214), and it loses QA to every source-free curve
(median 0.221) because it over-predicts the QA improvement. The
source-free median curve therefore has the lowest aggregate error
(0.237 against 0.386). Beyond the fitted range the picture is uniform:
at $d=0.55$ every source-conditioned form fails on every source tested
(errors 0.6 to 4.5 nats; the strength-only curve is closest), so the
transfer range of these relations is the interpolation regime.

\paragraph{Quantization settings.} The per-channel predictor has one
unseen setting, 5 bits, and its fixed rule (the mean of the committed
4- and 6-bit predictions) is worse than a per-bit constant on the 1B
source. With group size as a second axis the outcome changes. At the
unseen bit-width $b=4$ on the six development states, the
two-dimensional form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:quant2d} has MAE 0.19, 0.21,
and 0.44 against 0.45, 0.53, and 0.48 for zero change and 0.55, 0.73,
and 0.54 for the per-configuration median; at the unseen group size
$g=128$ across three bit-widths it has 0.22, 0.32, and 0.99 against
1.24, 1.35, and 1.24 for zero change, on all three capabilities. The
separable candidate is worse than zero change on the bit test and the
bilinear interpolation fails outside the development corners, so the
response does not factor into a state amplitude and a shared shape.
These are configuration transfers on seen states; the one new state
tested (1B@96k at $g=128$, three cells) is mixed, with the
two-dimensional form best on code and behind the source-free curves on
math and QA.

\paragraph{Distillation pools and budgets.} The four forms frozen on
the endpoint pools U75 and U600 were applied to an unseen pool U225
whose reuse count (2.3 to 4.7) lies inside the endpoint range and
whose sampling protocol differs (random sample against pool prefix).
The reuse-count form beats the constant only for QA (0.706 against
1.449); for math and code the constant is best (0.122 and 0.069
against 0.191 and 0.117) and the fitted forms over-extrapolate. The
residual is dominated by systematic bias, $-0.87$ nats at one budget
and $+0.48$ at the other for QA, and variation across pool samples
exceeds variation across training seeds (0.43 against 0.04 nats).
Under the absolute-exposure protocol, predictions of eight forms for
three students on three unseen U375 pools were frozen before those runs
began; their measurement was in progress at the data cut-off of this
version and is not reported here.

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

\paragraph{Unseen stage inside the range.} At step 96k, between the
development stages, the frozen per-density pruning regression beat
both its no-$D_0$ variant and the per-setting median on all three
capabilities for the 160M and 1.4B sources (0.090, 0.128, 0.426
against 0.367, 0.438, 0.832 for the median). The per-bit quantization
regression on three sources at the same stage gains nothing over the
per-bit median at four bits and above, where responses are near zero,
and is inconsistent at int3 ($+0.45$ for math, $-1.12$ for code,
$-0.32$ for QA).

\paragraph{New size inside the range.} For a 1B source at steps 32k
and 112k, at interpolated densities, the power form, A2, and the
source-free median curve are within 0.05 nats of one another on every
capability; per stage, A2 is the best predictor at 112k on each
capability and the median curve is best at 32k, driven by QA. A new
in-range size is therefore predicted about as well by the source
relation as by the development median.

\paragraph{A source five times larger.} For a 6.9B source the
source-conditioned pruning forms lose to the median curve on math
(0.30 against 0.12) and QA (0.91 against 0.12) and tie on code: the
regression extends the panel's size trend into QA improvements of one
to three nats that do not occur, and over-predicts math and code
damage by about a factor of two at mid densities. Fitting on early
stages only (protocol B) brings the errors to the level of the
source-free curves without beating them. The per-bit quantization
regression is worse than the per-bit median or zero change at every
bit-width for this source, by 0.1 to 0.4 nats above 3 bits and by 1.2
to 3.6 nats at int3, with one protocol-B exception at int3 (1.29
against 3.8 to 4.1). On the third new checkpoint of the confirmation
panel, 6.9B@80k at interpolated densities, the delivered power form
ties the median curve on math (0.30 against 0.31) and loses on code
and QA, so the size boundary is soft for math and hard for QA.

\paragraph{New students.} Predictions for Gemma-3-4B as a held-out
student, at the development pools and at the unseen pools, are frozen
and their measurement was in progress at the cut-off.

\subsection{Applicability across Capabilities}
\label{sec:capabilities}

The same asymmetry recurs on every axis. Math and code are carried by
the initial-state inputs: the dense anchor has interval-supported
value for them, the pruning forms predict them at unseen densities and
stages, the two-dimensional quantization form predicts them at unseen
settings, and the distillation source form predicts math. QA is not:
its pruning response is over-predicted by every source-conditioned
form on every new checkpoint, a constant or a source-free curve is
better for it on both distillation axes except the reuse-count form on
the unseen pool, and its quantization error is the largest of the
three on every test. Capability conditioning is also needed in the
response shape, not only in the amplitude: on the complete
distillation development matrix, per-capability models with the same
parameter count as a shared-shape model lower the held-out error by
0.1 to 0.4 nats on every capability for the budget-plus-reuse
structures, and only marginally for the reuse-only structure
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_forms}). Code results carry the caveat of
\S\ref{sec:loss}: the within-capability specificity of the code loss
was not detected on the secondary benchmark.
