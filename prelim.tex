\section{Setup: Objects, Basic Parameters, and Observables}
\label{sec:prelim}

Scaling laws earn their usefulness from the discipline of their setup:
a small set of \emph{basic parameters} anyone can plug in, a continuous
observable with known statistics, and a fixed fitting-and-validation
protocol declared before the fits. This section fixes all three for
scaling-down.

\subsection{Objects}

A \emph{source model} has weights $\wz$, family $f$, and parameter
count $N_0$. A \emph{compression operator} $\comp{m}{s}$ maps the
source to a smaller or cheaper model, where
$m\in\{\text{prune},\text{quant},\text{distill}\}$ and $s$ is the
method's strength. A \emph{recovery operator} $R_{\Dr}$ performs
$\Dr$ tokens of continued training on the compressed model. For
same-architecture methods the induced weight perturbation is
$\dw=\comp{m}{s}(\wz)-\wz$: magnitude pruning at density $\dens$ gives
$\dw=-\wz\odot\maskdens_{\dens}$ (masking the $(1-\dens)$ fraction of
smallest-magnitude weights), and round-to-nearest quantization at
bit-width $\bits$ gives the rounding error. Distillation replaces the
architecture: a student of size $N_S$ is trained on $\Ds$ tokens of
teacher traces under a \emph{recipe} $\rho$ (which parts of the trace
supervise the student).

\subsection{Observables: a capability vector, measured in loss}
\label{sec:observables}

We fix a capability set $\capset$ (here mathematical reasoning, code
generation, multi-hop QA; the protocol extends to further capabilities)
and give each capability $c$ a \emph{probe set} $\probeset{c}$ of
prompt--reference pairs. The primary observable is the
capability-conditional task loss
\begin{equation}
\Lc{c}(w) \;=\; \frac{1}{T_c}\sum_{(x,y)\in\probeset{c}}
\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} -\log p_w\!\left(y_t \mid x, y_{<t}\right),
\label{eq:taskloss}
\end{equation}
the mean cross-entropy over reference-completion tokens. Three
protocol rules, each forced by evidence:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Continuous loss, not accuracy.} Downstream metrics are
predictable essentially only through continuous log-likelihood
measurements \citep{schaeffer2023emergent,schaeffer2024predicting,
gadre2024language,bhagia2024establishing}; laws are fit in $\vec{L}$
space, and task success enters only through a separately measured,
monotone saturating link $A_c=g_c(\Lc{c})$.
\item \textbf{Cross-benchmark probes.} Probe benchmarks are disjoint
at the \emph{benchmark} level (not merely the split level) from all
transfer and recovery data. Our re-analysis of a prior evaluation
(\S\ref{sec:reanalysis}) found that same-benchmark evaluation inflated
apparent distillation gains on QA by roughly one third.
\item \textbf{Split estimation from measurement.} Each probe set is
halved once; geometry statistics are estimated on one half, compressed
models are measured on the other, so predictions and measurements never
share sampling noise. Latent-ability (IRT) summaries, when used at all,
are restricted to rank statements (discrimination heterogeneity leaves
rankings intact, $\rho\ge0.98$, but voids interval-scale use).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Target}

A \emph{scaling-down law} is a predictive map
\begin{equation}
(N_0, f, m, s, \Dr) \;\longmapsto\; \vec{L}=\left(\Lc{c}\right)_{c\in\capset},
\label{eq:sdlaw}
\end{equation}
reported in two forms: a \emph{mechanism form} whose coefficients are
independently measured functionals of the source model
(\S\ref{sec:method}), and a \emph{reduced form} in the basic parameters
of Table~\ref{tab:params} alone. Validation is by held-out
extrapolation---largest size, deepest compression, largest recovery
budget---never by in-sample fit quality.
