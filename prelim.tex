\section{Preliminaries and Measurement Layer}
\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Objects.}
A \emph{source model} has weights $\wz$, family $f$, and parameter count
$N_0$. A \emph{compression operator} $\comp{m}{s}$ maps the source to a
smaller or cheaper model, where $m\in\{\text{prune},\text{quant},
\text{distill}\}$ and $s$ is the method's strength parameter: remaining
density $\dens\in(0,1]$ for magnitude pruning, bit-width $\bits$ for
post-training quantization, and student size $N_S$ with transfer set size
$\Ds$ and recipe for distillation. A \emph{recovery operator} $R_{\Dr}$
performs $\Dr$ tokens of continued training on a compressed model. For
same-architecture methods (pruning, quantization) we write the induced
weight perturbation $\dw = \comp{m}{s}(\wz)-\wz$; for pruning
$\dw=-\wz\odot \maskdens_{\dens}$ where $\maskdens_{\dens}$ masks the
$(1-\dens)$ fraction of smallest-magnitude weights, and for round-to-nearest
quantization $\dw$ is the rounding error.

\paragraph{Capability vector.}
We fix a capability set $\capset$ (here: mathematical reasoning, code
generation, multi-hop QA; the framework extends to knowledge,
instruction-following, and tool use). Each capability $c$ has a
\emph{probe set} $\probeset{c}$ of prompt--reference pairs drawn from
benchmarks that are \emph{cross-benchmark disjoint} from any data used for
distillation or recovery---a hard constraint motivated by our re-analysis of
prior evaluations, in which same-benchmark evaluation inflated apparent
distillation gains on QA by roughly one third (\S\ref{sec:exp}). The primary
observable is the \emph{capability-conditional task loss}
\begin{equation}
\Lc{c}(w) \;=\; \frac{1}{T_c}\sum_{(x,y)\in\probeset{c}}
\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} -\log p_w\!\left(y_t \mid x, y_{<t}\right),
\label{eq:taskloss}
\end{equation}
the mean cross-entropy over reference-completion tokens ($T_c$ is the total
token count). Being an average over thousands of tokens, $\Lc{c}$ is a
low-variance, continuous measurement; this choice follows the evidence that
discrete accuracy metrics destroy the statistical regularity needed for
extrapolation \citep{schaeffer2024predicting,gadre2024language,
bhagia2024establishing}. Probe sets are split once into an
\emph{estimation half} (used for measuring geometry, \S\ref{sec:method}) and
a \emph{measurement half} (used for evaluating compressed models), so
predictions and measurements never share sampling noise.

\paragraph{Link layer.}
Task success is a separately measured, capability-specific link
$A_c = g_c(\Lc{c})$ with pre-registered qualitative constraints
(monotone, saturating at both ends). Item-level responses are retained for
diagnostics, but no law is fit in accuracy space or in latent-ability (IRT)
space; when an IRT summary is used at all, it is restricted to rank-level
statements, since discrimination heterogeneity invalidates interval-scale
use while leaving rankings intact (Spearman $\rho\ge 0.98$ between 1PL and
2PL abilities in our re-analysis).

\paragraph{Target laws.}
A \emph{scaling-down law} is a predictive map
\begin{equation}
(N_0, f, m, s, \Dr) \;\longmapsto\; \vec{L} = \left(\Lc{c}\right)_{c\in\capset},
\label{eq:sdlaw}
\end{equation}
reported in two forms: a \emph{mechanism form}, whose terms are functionals
of measured capability geometry, and a \emph{reduced form} containing only
basic parameters, obtained by fitting the geometry statistics themselves as
smooth functions of $N_0$. Only the reduced form is needed to use the law.
