\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

The experiments follow the prediction problem of \S\ref{sec:prelim}. We
first describe the panel and protocol (\S\ref{sec:setup}), then the
capability-conditioned responses of the heterogeneous panel under
pruning (\S\ref{sec:curves}), quantization (\S\ref{sec:quant}), and
distillation (\S\ref{sec:distill}), and then the controlled two-axis
prediction tests that carry the main claims (\S\ref{sec:twoaxes}), ending
with the limitations that remain (\S\ref{sec:planned}). Mechanism evidence
(capability regions and selective ablation, the gradient-alignment law,
the signed first-order term, the geometric cliff order parameter), the
recovery arm, scale and family structure, and the re-analysis of the
prior ability-space grid are supporting material and are given in full in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_support}.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Models.} Twelve dense checkpoints from four families: Qwen3
\{0.6B, 1.7B, 4B\} (measured before a cluster-policy change restricted
further use; retained as a comparison family), Gemma-3 \{270M, 1B, 4B,
12B, 27B\}, Gemma-4-31B, Muse-Glimmer-30B, OLMo-3 \{7B, 32B\}.
\textbf{Probes.} Three capabilities (math, code, multi-hop QA), each
probed by held-out splits of benchmarks cross-benchmark disjoint from
all transfer data; for capability-region tests, independent benchmarks per capability
(math: GSM8K/MATH-500/SVAMP; QA: HotpotQA/2Wiki/TriviaQA; code:
HumanEval/MBPP, a third planned) plus a C4 control.
\textbf{Compression.} Global magnitude pruning (ten densities,
$\dens\in[0.3,0.9]$); per-output-channel round-to-nearest weight
quantization ($\bits\in\{8,6,4,3\}$); black-box distillation from two
frontier API teachers onto Gemma-3 students; LoRA recovery training on
general (C4) or teacher-trace data. \textbf{Geometry.} Estimated as in
\S\ref{sec:geometry}; estimation and measurement probe halves are
disjoint throughout, and a checkpoint-integrity guard aborts any run
with missing or re-initialized weights.

\subsection{Quantization arm}
\label{sec:quant}

Across nine models measured at $\bits\in\{8,6,4,3\}$: int8 and int6
are within noise of dense everywhere; int4 damages small models
(Gemma3-270M math $+2.3$) and barely touches large ones
(Gemma3-27B math $+0.33$); and $\bits=3$ collapses each model we measured ($+5$ to $+18$ nats).
In leave-one-bit-out validation a learned exponential $q_ce^{-k\bits}$
beats the fixed $q_c4^{-\bits}$ candidate (pooled MAE $6.73$ vs.\
$7.63$): bit decay is exponential, with a fitted base.
Capability selectivity transfers to this basis: QA improves under
int4 for Qwen3-1.7B ($-0.21$) and Gemma3-27B ($-0.67$), both clearing
the mildest-perturbation noise screen ($29$ of $62$ nominal
improvement cells across all arms survive that screen; the rest are
within noise and not claimed).

\subsection{Distillation arm}
\label{sec:distill}

Black-box traces from two frontier teachers (GPT-5.6-luna and
Claude-Sonnet-4-6; 600 per domain each; single-teacher runs in a paired
design) train Gemma-3 students under coverage recipes. The paired
results are directionally consistent with the pre-registered
coverage-deletion prediction: the answer-only recipe hurts math more
than full-trace training ($+0.19$ to $+0.22$ vs.\ $+0.09$ to
$+0.12$), while QA improves in each configuration ($-0.58$ to
$-1.44$). We do not attribute that gap to reasoning-coverage loss on
this evidence alone, because the recipes differ in supervised tokens
and update counts; the controlled comparisons that hold those
quantities fixed are the reuse-count and pool experiments of
\S\ref{sec:twoaxes}, where the response is modeled as a signed
transfer relative to the student's own dense loss.

\subsection{Controlled prediction on two axes}
\label{sec:twoaxes}

The heterogeneous panel above tests how far a law transfers across
finished models; it cannot separate the effect of training history from
that of architecture and data, because those co-vary across public
families. We add a controlled panel, Pythia \{160M, 410M,
1.4B\} at pretraining steps \{16k, 64k, 143k\} (33.6B, 134B, and 300B
tokens), on which the source state $\mathbf x_0=(N_0,D_0,L_{c,0})$
varies along known axes, and we ask two questions separately: whether
source-state information predicts the compression response at a fixed
intervention (the source axis), and whether a relation fit at seen
intervention strengths predicts unseen ones (the strength axis).
Table~\ref{tab:main} collects each registered test. Predictions marked
P were frozen before the corresponding measurement, and P-new were
frozen and committed before a new source was measured; L are
leave-one-out on an existing panel. R marks two outcomes with the same
standing: a frozen candidate that did not beat its strongest
same-budget baseline, and a baseline added at closeout with the original
prediction untouched. All three arms use the same K0 budget
(pre-compression information only), the same endpoint, and the same
rule: a candidate is credited only against the strongest baseline at
the same information budget, that is, one built from the same inputs.

\paragraph{Source axis.}
Three statements are kept separate here: whether adding $D_0$ improves
on the model without it, whether the full-input model improves on the
strongest simple baseline, and whether a frozen model succeeds on a new
source state. On the $3\times3$ panel, adding $D_0$ to $\{N_0, L_{c,0}\}$ lowers
held-out error in 4 of 12 arm$\times$capability$\times$split comparisons
with a bootstrap interval excluding zero, for math and code but not QA.
Against the strongest simple baseline, a per-configuration median, the
full predictor wins with an interval excluding zero only for code; for
math the point improvement is positive but the interval includes zero.
The gain concentrates in aggressive settings (pruning $d=0.6$, int3),
and for the reported capabilities and splits it is absent when the
most-trained checkpoint is held out; we do not generalize this to all
latest-stage extrapolation. Because the predictor uses configuration
indicators, it is a source-transfer model at a fixed density or
bit-width, not a law over the intervention axis. On a training stage that had not entered any fit (step 96k, a frozen prospective stage interpolation
between 64k and 143k), the frozen pruning predictor beat both the
baseline and the
no-$D_0$ model for all three capabilities (math 0.090 vs.\ 0.367; code
0.128 vs.\ 0.438; QA 0.426 vs.\ 0.832), whereas the quantization
predictor was mixed (math and QA above baseline, code below), a pattern
the partition analysis below explains.

\paragraph{Pruning: strength axis.}
A shared-shape candidate
$\widehat{\Delta L}_c(\mathbf x,d)=A_c(\mathbf x)\,((1-d)/0.3)^{\gamma_c}$
with source-conditioned amplitude
$A_c(\mathbf x)=\boldsymbol\beta_c\cdot(1,\log N_0,L_{c,0},\log D_0)$
was frozen on the panel at $d\in\{0.9,0.8,0.7,0.6\}$ and evaluated at
unseen $d=0.65$ (interpolation) and $d=0.55$ (deeper extrapolation),
including a new source (410M at step 96k). It beats each
strength-only baseline by a wide margin (MAE 0.371, 0.524, and 0.759
for math, code, and QA against 1.37, 1.63, and 1.61 for a strength-only
curve), because a source-blind curve cannot see that one state (160M at
step 143k) is several times more fragile than another. Two closeout
baselines at the same information budget, however, change what this
shows. A per-density source regression with linear interpolation and
extrapolation in $d$, which uses the same inputs and no shared exponent,
has better point estimates than the candidate for math (0.283 vs.\
0.371) and code (0.279 vs.\ 0.524) and is within noise for QA (0.818
vs.\ 0.759); whether these differences are significant is not
established on a panel of three sources. The candidate beats only the
variant with $\gamma_c$ fixed to one. On the tested panel,
the source-informed candidate outperforms strength-only baselines, and
the added same-input comparison shows no additional advantage for the
shared power form. This does not mean that only the inputs matter,
since the per-density baseline also uses the response structure across
the measured strengths. At $d=0.55$ the signed
errors are mixed (six under-predictions, three over-predictions), with
the most fragile source over-predicted, so we do not describe the deep
regime as systematically under-predicted. QA amplitude does not follow
the math/code relation.

\paragraph{Quantization: partition.}
The tested interventions are the integer bit-widths $\{8,6,4,3\}$;
there is no integer point between 3 and 4, so the strength-axis test
available here is per configuration. On three new sources at step 96k,
responses at 4 bits and above are near zero and candidate and
per-bit-median errors are both small (math 0.029 vs.\ 0.084; code
0.104 vs.\ 0.094; QA 0.173 vs.\ 0.134), so no incremental value over a
simple baseline is demonstrated at that error scale, which is not
evidence that none exists. The int3 responses are real collapse, and the
candidate advantage there is unstable across sources ($+0.45$ for math,
$-1.12$ for code, and $-0.32$ for QA relative to the median), so
magnitude transfer at int3 is not established. The fixed $4^{-b}$ shape
misfits under this quantizer definition, and a learned exponential fits
better within the tested range (\S\ref{sec:quant}). We claim no
collapse-risk law.

\paragraph{New-source frozen prospective (pruning and quantization).}
To test the retrospective same-input comparison on an independent
source, we froze and committed each candidate's prediction for
Pythia-1B at step 96k, a new size inside the 160M--1.4B range at an
interpolated training stage that had not entered any fit or
selection, before measuring it, using only its measured dense loss and
public descriptors. At the interpolated density $d=0.65$ the
source-conditioned forms succeed and are indistinguishable (MAE 0.149
for the $\gamma=1$ variant, 0.172 for the per-density baseline, and
0.193 for the shared power form, against 0.704 for strength-only and
0.657 for zero change), with math and code errors of 0.02 to 0.09 and
QA the weakest capability. At the extrapolated density $d=0.55$ each
source-conditioned candidate fails (1.14 to 1.69), under-predicting the
collapse of this source by 1 to 1.5 nats and missing the sign of the QA
response, while the source-blind strength-only curve is closest (0.273);
the transfer range of the source-conditioned family appears to be the
interpolation regime. For quantization the frozen full-input predictor
is best at both bits on this source (int4 MAE 0.074 against 0.211
without $D_0$ and 0.111 for the per-bit median; int3 0.397 against
0.620 and 2.46), so $D_0$ carries information here. This is one
source-state: it confirms or refutes transfer for that state only and
supports no population claim.

\paragraph{Second round: two training stages of one new source.}
A second frozen round measured the same 1B size at steps 32k and 112k
on seven densities and five bit-widths, with predictions committed
before measurement (Table~\ref{tab:p1v2}, Appendix~\ref{sec:app_p1v2}).
At the late stage the per-density source regression is the best pruning
predictor in the interpolation regime under both fitting protocols (MAE
0.112 against 0.173 for the source-free median curve and 0.365 for
strength-only when the full panel is used for fitting), and it leads on
each capability separately. At the early stage its advantage is not
robust. The median curve is better when the full panel is used (0.204
against 0.261), mostly because of QA, and the source regression leads
only narrowly when fitting is restricted to steps at or below 64k.
Extrapolation to $d=0.55$ fails again for the source-conditioned
family, with strength-only closest under the full-panel protocol. For
quantization the full-input predictor pays off at 3 bits only (0.68 and
1.05 against 1.2 to 3.5 for the per-bit median); from 4 bits upward the
per-bit median is as good or better, and the 5-bit interpolation rule is
worse than the median. We read this as stage-dependent transfer: the
source signal appears informative once the source is well trained, and
the early-stage QA response is the part the current form misses.

\paragraph{Size extrapolation to 6.9B.}
The same freeze covered Pythia-6.9B at steps 32k and 112k, about five
times the largest development size. Here the source-conditioned family
fails. With the full panel used for fitting, the per-density regression
has interpolation MAE 0.48 and 0.46 at the two stages, against 0.17 and
0.09 for the source-free median curve and 0.16 and 0.29 for zero change
(Table~\ref{tab:p1v2}). Most of the error is QA: the regression extends
the panel's size trend into QA improvements of one to three nats,
whereas the measured response stays below 0.65 nats and turns into a
1.45 nat loss at $d=0.55$ for the late stage. Math and code damage is
over-predicted by about a factor of two at mid densities, so the larger
source is more robust per density than the trend below 1.4B suggests.
Restricting the fit to early development stages brings the regression
to 0.17 and 0.11, on par with strength-only and still behind the median
curve. The quantization class-indicator candidate does not transfer
either. From 4 bits upward the per-bit median is better by a factor of
six or more, and at 3 bits the candidate overshoots by about four nats.
Taken together with the 1B pair, the current source-conditioned
predictors transfer within roughly the development size range and the
interpolation density range, and not beyond either.

\paragraph{Distillation: source and pool axes.}
With the reference fixed to the initial student,
$\delta_c=L_c(S_{\mathrm{KD}})-L_c(S_0)$, a controlled LoRA panel (the
same $3\times3$ sources with fixed teacher, pool, and reuse count
$E=T/D_U$, the ratio of processed tokens to pool tokens)
admits a source-transfer predictor only for math, where a linear model
in $\{N_0,L_{c,0},D_0\}$ halves the held-out error of a constant (0.014
vs.\ 0.031); for code the two are within noise (0.057 vs.\ 0.061) and
for QA a per-capability constant is better (0.159 vs.\ 0.263). On the pool axis, predictors frozen on the endpoint pools (U75,
U600) were tested on an unseen middle pool (U225). Its reuse count of
2.3--4.7 lies inside the endpoint range, so this is a configuration
interpolation, and because the endpoints are pool prefixes while U225 is
randomly sampled, the sampling protocol also changes. The reuse-count predictor
beats a constant only for QA (0.706 vs.\ 1.449); for math and code a
constant is best (0.122 and 0.069) and the endpoint-fit forms
over-extrapolate. All four frozen candidates (a constant, a budget-only
form, a reuse-only form, and a two-variable form) were registered
before the U225 measurement; the per-capability recommendation of
reuse-only for QA and a constant for math and code is a selection made
after seeing those results and is labeled as such in
Table~\ref{tab:main}. The prediction residual is dominated by systematic
bias, with little from pool variation: math and code show a small
over-prediction bias with little spread, and the QA reuse-count
predictor carries a bias that changes sign across the two budgets
($-0.87$ then $+0.48$), a form misfit more than pool sensitivity.
Around that bias, variation across pools exceeds variation across
training seeds, but with three pools and two seeds this describes the
design and does not decompose a population variance, and we make no
causal claim about the residual.

\paragraph{Reading across arms.}
The three arms share the prediction endpoint, the information budget,
and the validation standard, and the response form and its range of
validity are determined for each arm by evidence: pruning yields a
source-conditioned prediction that extends to unseen densities,
quantization a per-configuration validation whose only large signal is
an unstable collapse magnitude, and distillation a capability-specific
transfer in which the reuse-count predictor helps QA and a constant
suffices for math and code. The cross-method tests reported here cover the
resource-ratio models of \S\ref{sec:claims} at matched storage and
active-parameter coordinates; the full M1/M2/M3 held-out comparison was
not completed (\S\ref{sec:planned}). Whether any single response form
unifies the arms remains open. How these predictors would
enter a capability- and cost-aware method-selection map is discussed in
\S\ref{sec:map}; the existing Pareto and mis-ranking analyses are
implications of prediction error, not a validated selector.

\subsection{Remaining limitations}
\label{sec:planned}

The controlled panels have three size, step, and pool clusters, so all
intervals are panel-conditional and should not be read as population estimates. The
shared pruning form is not independently necessary, since a per-density
fit ties it. No held-out agent or method-selection validation was run,
so the selection map remains a framework deliverable and not a validated
selector. The full capacity$\times$teacher$\times$data distillation
grid, large-model geometric order parameters, the cross-method M1/M2/M3
held-out comparison of \S\ref{sec:claims}, agent-workload selection
validation, and the held-out extrapolation battery for each reduced
form (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) were not completed in this work and are
stated as such.
