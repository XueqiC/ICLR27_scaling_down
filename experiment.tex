\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

The experiments build the argument in logical order: capabilities
occupy distinct, causally implicated regions of the gradient space of
the source model (\S\ref{sec:regions}); pruning damage follows a law whose
coefficients are carried by that geometry (\S\ref{sec:curves}--%
\S\ref{sec:law}); the signed first-order term predicts
capability-selective effects in both directions across families
(\S\ref{sec:mech}); the behavioral cliff, non-perturbative in loss,
becomes a smooth threshold crossing in geometry
(\S\ref{sec:orderparam}); the quantization, distillation, and recovery
arms extend the same structure to the remaining deletion bases and to
the framework's single addition operator
(\S\ref{sec:quant}--\S\ref{sec:recovery}); and the family axis exposes
which quantities are universal and which are family coefficients
(\S\ref{sec:family}). A forensic re-analysis of a prior ability-space
grid (\S\ref{sec:reanalysis}) motivated the measurement protocol.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Models.} Twelve dense checkpoints from four families: Qwen3
\{0.6B, 1.7B, 4B\} (measured before a cluster-policy change restricted
further use; retained as a comparison family), Gemma-3 \{270M, 1B, 4B,
12B, 27B\}, Gemma-4-31B, Muse-Glimmer-30B, OLMo-3 \{7B, 32B\}.
\textbf{Probes.} Three capabilities (math, code, multi-hop QA), each
probed by held-out splits of benchmarks cross-benchmark disjoint from
all transfer data; for capability-region tests, independent benchmarks per capability
(math: GSM8K/MATH-500/SVAMP; QA: HotpotQA/2Wiki/TriviaQA; code:
HumanEval/MBPP, a third planned) plus a C4 control.
\textbf{Compression.} Global magnitude pruning (ten densities,
$\dens\in[0.3,0.9]$); per-output-channel round-to-nearest weight
quantization ($\bits\in\{8,6,4,3\}$); black-box distillation from two
frontier API teachers onto Gemma-3 students; LoRA recovery training on
general (C4) or teacher-trace data. \textbf{Geometry.} Estimated as in
\S\ref{sec:geometry}; estimation and measurement probe halves are
disjoint throughout, and a checkpoint-integrity guard aborts any run
with missing or re-initialized weights.

\subsection{Capabilities occupy distinct regions of gradient space}
\label{sec:regions}

\paragraph{Convergent validity (block structure).} For each of nine
benchmarks we measure a gradient signature (the benchmark-conditional
diagonal Fisher) and compare all pairs. After removing the component
shared across benchmarks, signatures of same-capability benchmarks are
positively aligned (cosine $+0.36$ to $+0.45$) while signatures of
different capabilities are \emph{anti-aligned} ($-0.23$ to $-0.25$),
in every one of seven models across three families
(Fig.~\ref{fig:blocks}); the C4 control sits between blocks.
\preliminary{point estimates; example-bootstrap, permutation, and
prompt-stability tests are queued (B2)} The
mathematics benchmarks cluster regardless of format, and within-block
fine structure matches intuition (SVAMP, grade-school word problems,
is the loosest member of the math block). A projected-gradient SVD
version gives the same verdict in subspace terms: principal-angle
similarity within capability exceeds cross-capability similarity by
$2$--$4\times$.

\subsection{The pruning law}
\label{sec:law}

The mechanism form of \S\ref{sec:laws} fits the entire pre-cliff grid.
With the measured alignment term subtracted, the curvature residual
follows a power law in the deleted capability mass $m_c(\dens)$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Lc{c}(\dens)\;=\;\underbrace{\gbar{c}^{\top}\dw_\dens}_{\text{measured}}
\;+\;B\,\big[\underbrace{m_c(\dens)}_{\text{measured}}\big]^{\gamma},
\label{eq:fitlaw}
\end{equation}
with in-sample $R^2 = 0.82$--$0.996$ over 24 capability$\times$model
fits. The held-out battery (1000-resample cell bootstrap) is where the
form proves itself: in \emph{leave-one-family-out} transfer the
shared-$\gamma$ mechanism form attains MAE $2.17$ nats versus $10.3$
for per-capability raw sparsity and $8.2$ for the direct-damage
baseline (both CIs exclude zero), so the deleted-mass coordinate is what
transfers across families; it also beats the direct-damage baseline in
fit-shallow/predict-deep ($3.11$ vs.\ $4.24$) and raw sparsity in
leave-largest-model-out ($1.41$ vs.\ $2.15$), while its edge over raw
sparsity within-model does not reach significance
(without the first-order term, QA is unfittable, $R^2\!\le\!0.67$;
with it, QA joins the law). Two structural results:
(i) in the deleted-mass coordinate the exponent concentrates at
$\gamma\in[1.2,2.2]$ across all families, versus $3.2$--$8.7$ in the
raw $(1-\dens)$ coordinate, which is what makes
the form portable; and (ii) \emph{within a model, $\gamma$ is shared
across capabilities} (e.g.\ Qwen3-0.6B: $1.41/1.56/1.43$;
Gemma3-4B: $1.66/1.71$): capability differences are carried entirely
by the two measured quantities, and the fitted structure is two
parameters per model. Published (reduced) forms substitute the
smooth dependence of the geometry itself on $(N_0,f)$, leaving only basic
parameters in the interface.

\subsection{The signed first-order term predicts, in both directions}
\label{sec:mech}

The alignment term $\gbar{c}^{\top}\dw$ is the decisive family
coefficient. Its sign predicts the presence or absence of the
capability-selective ``free lunch'' in every case measured:
the QA term of Qwen3 is negative and QA improves under pruning (predicted
$-0.83$ vs.\ measured $-0.79$ at 4B, $\dens=0.7$); the QA term of Gemma is
positive and no improvement exists at any size; the term for OLMo-3-7B is
positive (mild degradation, prediction $+0.015$ $=$ measurement at
$\dens=0.8$, code) while the term for OLMo-3-32B is negative, and
deep-pruning QA improvement duly appears. At the level of individual model$\times$capability$\times$density
cells in the mild regime, sign prediction accuracy is $67.6\%$
(Wilson 95\% CI $[58.3,75.7]$, $n{=}108$), above chance, with the
family-level pattern (Qwen and OLMo-32B QA improvements, Gemma
none, an OLMo size flip) called in advance. Beyond the mild regime, first- plus second-order
predictions fail by orders of magnitude at the cliff in every family:
the collapse is non-perturbative in loss space, confirming the
two-regime structure of \eqref{eq:prune}.

\subsection{The cliff is a geometric threshold crossing}
\label{sec:orderparam}

Measuring the capability geometry \emph{of the pruned models}
resolves the apparent unpredictability of the cliff. The
capability-region mass retention (the fraction of the
capability-exclusive top coordinates of the dense model still
prominent after pruning) declines smoothly from the first densities
(no cliff in geometry) while loss is still flat;
the behavioral cliff occurs where retention crosses a critical value.
The critical value is family-specific (Gemma: $0.035$--$0.10$ across
1B/4B and all capabilities; Qwen: $0.13$--$0.23$) but consistent
within a family across sizes and capabilities: the cliff boundary
$\dens_c^{*}(N_0,f)$ reduces to one critical-retention constant per
family, turning the least elegant fitted parameter of the two-regime
law into a measurable, smoothly evolving order parameter.

\subsection{Quantization arm}
\label{sec:quant}

Across nine models measured at $\bits\in\{8,6,4,3\}$: int8 and int6
are within noise of dense everywhere; int4 damages small models
(Gemma3-270M math $+2.3$) and barely touches large ones
(Gemma3-27B math $+0.33$); and $\bits=3$ collapses every model measured ($+5$ to $+18$ nats).
In leave-one-bit-out validation a learned exponential $q_ce^{-k\bits}$
beats the fixed $q_c4^{-\bits}$ candidate (pooled MAE $6.73$ vs.\
$7.63$): bit decay is exponential, with a fitted base.
Capability selectivity transfers to this basis: QA improves under
int4 for Qwen3-1.7B ($-0.21$) and Gemma3-27B ($-0.67$), both clearing
the mildest-perturbation noise screen ($29$ of $62$ nominal
improvement cells across all arms survive that screen; the rest are
within noise and not claimed).

\subsection{Distillation arm (in progress)}
\label{sec:distill}

Black-box traces from two frontier teachers (GPT-5.6-luna,
Claude-Sonnet-4-6; 600 per domain each; single-teacher runs, paired
design) train Gemma-3 students under coverage recipes. Early paired
results are directionally consistent with the pre-registered
coverage-deletion prediction: the answer-only recipe hurts math more
than full-trace training ($+0.19$ to $+0.22$ vs.\ $+0.09$ to
$+0.12$), while QA improves in every configuration ($-0.58$ to
$-1.44$). \preliminary{token-matched and update-matched controls are
required before attributing the gap to reasoning-coverage loss; the
full capacity$\times$teacher$\times$coverage$\times\Ds$ grid with
those controls is running}

\subsection{Recovery arm: redeemable damage and the asymptotic residual}
\label{sec:recovery}

Recovery training on general text fits the recovery law cleanly
(Gemma3-270M pruned to $\dens=0.6$; $R^2\approx1.0$):
$\beta'_{\text{math}}=1.23$, $\beta'_{\text{code}}=1.02$, with
\emph{asymptotic residuals under the fixed LoRA recipe},
$\Lc{c}^{\infty}-\Lc{c}^{\text{dense}}$, of $1.21$ (math) and $1.93$
(code) nats; about $80\%$ of this pruning damage is redeemed within
8M tokens and the remainder persists under this recipe, while QA
recovers completely. \preliminary{one model, one density, one recipe; with three-point
ladders the largest-budget extrapolation of the saturating form beats a
carry-forward baseline in only 4 of 9 fits, so the ladder is too sparse
to claim extrapolation, and the denser multi-model grid (B7) is
queued} Two mechanism results:
recovery on \emph{aligned} data (teacher traces) heals substantially
faster at small budgets (math $2.76$ vs.\ $3.47$ at $0.5$M tokens),
as the gradient-overlap prediction requires, but over-training on
narrow aligned data \emph{re-damages} the model (QA $4.95\to9.75$
from $0.5$M to $8$M tokens), while general-text recovery is monotone.
Aligned recovery is fast but risky; general recovery is slow but safe;
the optimal recovery budget is finite for narrow data. Quantization damage past the bit cliff is markedly less redeemable
under the same recipe (residuals $\approx2.6$--$2.8$ after int3) than
pruning damage at matched severity.

\subsection{Scale and family structure}
\label{sec:family}

\paragraph{Within-family robustness is non-monotonic in two families
independently.} Gemma-3 robustness peaks at 12B (27B collapses far
earlier, $+4.0$ vs.\ $+0.68$ at $\dens=0.7$), mirroring the prior
ability-space observation of a Qwen3 14B peak. A mid-scale robustness
peak is a reproducible pattern of scaling-down.
\paragraph{A candidate predictor, honestly retired.} The
top-concentration statistic $\kappa_c$ tracked robustness within the
Qwen3 ladder and was pre-registered as a predictor; the full grid
retires it (cross-family inversion: OLMo, the most robust family,
has the lowest concentration; within-Gemma top-end violation).
\paragraph{Matched-size panel ($\sim$30B).} At matched scale the
family coefficient dominates: $\Delta\Lc{\text{math}}$ at $\dens=0.7$
spans $+0.02$ (OLMo-3-32B), $+0.17$ (Muse-30B), $+0.68$ (Gemma4-31B),
$+4.04$ (Gemma3-27B). OLMo-3-32B survives $70\%$ pruning with
$\le+0.63$ nats on every capability. Family provenance (data
recency, over-training exposure) is a first-order determinant of
prunability, and ``which family should an agent scale down from?''
is a measured quantity.

\subsection{Re-analysis of the prior ability-space grid}
\label{sec:reanalysis}

A prior internal study fit compression laws directly on Rasch
abilities from $\sim$100-item panels over a 774-cell Qwen3 grid and
reported that no derived law had held-out predictive power. Our
re-analysis attributes much of this to the measurement layer:
(1) the $r{=}1$ falsification anchor was driven entirely by the
answer-only recipe (stratified, the anchor holds and held-out error
drops from $9.0\times$ to $1.8\times$ the noise floor); (2)
same-benchmark evaluation inflated QA gains by roughly one third;
(3) discrimination heterogeneity voids interval-scale ability while
rankings survive ($\rho\ge0.98$); (4) item-level damage is smooth
pre-cliff, ruling out discrete skill deletion at the item level.
These findings fixed the protocol of \S\ref{sec:prelim}.

\subsection{Controlled prediction on two axes}
\label{sec:twoaxes}

The heterogeneous panel above tests how far a law transfers across
finished models; it cannot separate the effect of training history from
that of architecture and data, because those co-vary across public
families. We therefore add a controlled panel, Pythia \{160M, 410M,
1.4B\} at pretraining steps \{16k, 64k, 143k\} (33.6B, 134B, and 300B
tokens), on which the source state $\mathbf x_0=(N_0,D_0,L_{c,0})$
varies along known axes, and we ask two questions separately: whether
source-state information predicts the compression response at a fixed
intervention (the source axis), and whether a relation fit at seen
intervention strengths predicts unseen ones (the strength axis).
Table~\ref{tab:main} collects every registered test. Predictions marked
P were frozen before the corresponding measurement, L are leave-one-out
on an existing panel, and R are baselines added at closeout with the
original prediction untouched. All three arms use the same K0 budget
(pre-compression information only), the same endpoint, and the same
rule: a candidate is credited only against the strongest baseline at
the same information budget.

\paragraph{Source axis.}
Three statements are kept separate here: whether adding $D_0$ improves
on the model without it, whether the full-input model improves on the
strongest simple baseline, and whether a frozen model succeeds on a new
source state. On the $3\times3$ panel, adding $D_0$ to $\{N_0, L_{c,0}\}$ lowers
held-out error in 4 of 12 arm$\times$capability$\times$split comparisons
with a bootstrap interval excluding zero, for math and code but not QA.
Against the strongest simple baseline, a per-configuration median, the
full predictor wins with an interval excluding zero only for code; for
math the point improvement is positive but the interval includes zero.
The gain concentrates in aggressive settings (pruning $d=0.6$, int3),
and for the reported capabilities and splits it is absent when the
most-trained checkpoint is held out; we do not generalize this to all
latest-stage extrapolation. Because the predictor uses configuration
indicators, it is a source-transfer model at a fixed density or
bit-width, not a law over the intervention axis. On a never-fit
training stage (step 96k, a frozen prospective stage interpolation
between 64k and 143k), the frozen pruning predictor beat both the
baseline and the
no-$D_0$ model for all three capabilities (math 0.090 vs.\ 0.367; code
0.128 vs.\ 0.438; QA 0.426 vs.\ 0.832), whereas the quantization
predictor was mixed (math and QA above baseline, code below), a pattern
the partition analysis below explains.

\paragraph{Pruning: strength axis.}
A shared-shape candidate
$\widehat{\Delta L}_c(\mathbf x,d)=A_c(\mathbf x)\,((1-d)/0.3)^{\gamma_c}$
with source-conditioned amplitude
$A_c(\mathbf x)=\boldsymbol\beta_c\cdot(1,\log N_0,L_{c,0},\log D_0)$
was frozen on the panel at $d\in\{0.9,0.8,0.7,0.6\}$ and evaluated at
unseen $d=0.65$ (interpolation) and $d=0.55$ (deeper extrapolation),
including a new source (410M at step 96k). It beats every
strength-only baseline by a wide margin (MAE 0.371, 0.524, and 0.759
for math, code, and QA against 1.37, 1.63, and 1.61 for a strength-only
curve), because a source-blind curve cannot see that one state (160M at
step 143k) is several times more fragile than another. Two closeout
baselines at the same information budget, however, change what this
shows. A per-density source regression with linear interpolation and
extrapolation in $d$, which uses the same inputs and no shared exponent,
has better point estimates than the candidate for math (0.283 vs.\
0.371) and code (0.279 vs.\ 0.524) and is within noise for QA (0.818
vs.\ 0.759); whether these differences are significant is not
established on a panel of three sources. The candidate beats only the
variant with $\gamma_c$ fixed to one. On the tested panel, therefore,
the source-informed candidate outperforms strength-only baselines, and
the added same-input comparison shows no additional advantage for the
shared power form. This does not reduce to inputs alone mattering,
since the per-density baseline also uses the response structure across
the measured strengths. At $d=0.55$ the signed
errors are mixed (six under-predictions, three over-predictions), with
the most fragile source over-predicted, so we do not describe the deep
regime as systematically under-predicted. QA amplitude does not follow
the math/code relation.

\paragraph{Quantization: partition.}
The tested interventions are the integer bit-widths $\{8,6,4,3\}$;
there is no integer point between 3 and 4, so the strength-axis test
available here is per configuration. On three new sources at step 96k,
responses at 4 bits and above are near zero and candidate and
per-bit-median errors are both small (math 0.029 vs.\ 0.084; code
0.104 vs.\ 0.094; QA 0.173 vs.\ 0.134), so no incremental value over a
simple baseline is demonstrated at that error scale, which is not
evidence that none exists. The int3 responses are real collapse, and the
candidate advantage there is unstable across sources ($+0.45$ for math,
$-1.12$ for code, and $-0.32$ for QA relative to the median), so
magnitude transfer at int3 is not established. The fixed $4^{-b}$ shape
misfits under this quantizer definition, and a learned exponential fits
better within the tested range (\S\ref{sec:quant}). We claim no
collapse-risk law.

\paragraph{Distillation: source and pool axes.}
With the reference fixed to the initial student,
$\delta_c=L_c(S_{\mathrm{KD}})-L_c(S_0)$, a controlled LoRA panel (the
same $3\times3$ sources with fixed teacher, pool, and reuse count)
admits a source-transfer predictor only for math, where a linear model
in $\{N_0,L_{c,0},D_0\}$ halves the held-out error of a constant (0.014
vs.\ 0.031); for code the two are within noise (0.057 vs.\ 0.061) and
for QA a per-capability constant is better (0.159 vs.\ 0.263). On the pool axis, predictors frozen on the endpoint pools (U75,
U600) were tested on an unseen middle pool (U225). Its reuse count of
2.3--4.7 lies inside the endpoint range, so this is a configuration
interpolation, and because the endpoints are pool prefixes while U225 is
randomly sampled, the sampling protocol also changes. A reuse-count law
beats a constant only for QA (0.706 vs.\ 1.449); for math and code a
constant is best (0.122 and 0.069) and the endpoint-fit forms
over-extrapolate. All four frozen candidates (a constant, a budget-only
form, a reuse-only form, and a two-variable form) were registered
before the U225 measurement; the per-capability recommendation of
reuse-only for QA and a constant for math and code is a selection made
after seeing those results and is labeled as such in
Table~\ref{tab:main}. The prediction residual is dominated by systematic
bias rather than by pool variation: math and code show a small
over-prediction bias with little spread, and the QA reuse-count
predictor carries a bias that changes sign across the two budgets
($-0.87$ then $+0.48$), a form misfit rather than pool sensitivity.
Around that bias, variation across pools exceeds variation across
training seeds, but with three pools and two seeds this describes the
design rather than decomposing a population variance, and we make no
causal claim about the residual.

\paragraph{Reading across arms.}
The three arms share the prediction endpoint, the information budget,
and the validation standard, and the response form and its range of
validity are determined for each arm by evidence: pruning yields a
source-conditioned prediction that extends to unseen densities,
quantization a per-configuration validation whose only large signal is
an unstable collapse magnitude, and distillation a capability-specific
transfer in which the reuse coordinate helps QA and a constant suffices
for math and code. The cross-method tests reported here cover the
resource-ratio models of \S\ref{sec:claims} at matched storage and
active-parameter coordinates; the full M1/M2/M3 held-out comparison was
not completed (\S\ref{sec:planned}). Whether any single response form
unifies the arms therefore remains open. How these predictors would
enter a capability- and cost-aware method-selection map is discussed in
\S\ref{sec:map}; the existing Pareto and mis-ranking analyses are
implications of prediction error, not a validated selector.

\subsection{Remaining limitations}
\label{sec:planned}

The controlled panels have three size, step, and pool clusters, so all
intervals are panel-conditional rather than population estimates. The
shared pruning form is not independently necessary, since a per-density
fit ties it. No held-out agent or method-selection validation was run,
so the selection map is a framework deliverable rather than a validated
selector. The full capacity$\times$teacher$\times$data distillation
grid, large-model geometric order parameters, the cross-method M1/M2/M3
held-out comparison of \S\ref{sec:claims}, agent-workload selection
validation, and the held-out extrapolation battery for every reduced
form (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) were not completed in this round and are
stated as such rather than filled in.
