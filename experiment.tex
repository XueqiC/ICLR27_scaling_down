\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

Following standard scaling-law methodology, we proceed from measurement
protocol (\S\ref{sec:setup}) to dense references and damage curves over a
model grid (\S\ref{sec:curves}), then to mechanism tests
(\S\ref{sec:mech}), invariances and their violations across scale and
family (\S\ref{sec:family}), and a matched-size cross-family panel
(\S\ref{sec:panel}). Throughout, the object under study is
\emph{scaling-down}: every curve describes what a source model loses, per
capability, as deletion deepens. A forensic re-analysis of a prior
ability-space grid (\S\ref{sec:reanalysis}) motivates the measurement
layer; the pre-registered distillation/quantization/recovery grid is in
progress (\S\ref{sec:planned}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Models.} Eleven dense checkpoints from four families:
Qwen3 \{0.6B, 1.7B, 4B\} (measured before a cluster-policy change
restricted further use; retained as a comparison family), Gemma-3
\{270M, 1B, 4B, 12B, 27B\}, Gemma-4-31B, Muse-Glimmer-30B, and
OLMo-3-7B (OLMo-3-32B in progress).
\textbf{Probes.} Per capability, prompt--reference pairs from held-out
benchmark splits that are cross-benchmark disjoint from all transfer
data: MATH-500 (math), MBPP (code), 2WikiMultihopQA (multi-hop QA);
64 estimation and 64 measurement samples per capability
(\S\ref{sec:prelim}).
\textbf{Compression.} Global magnitude pruning at ten densities
$\dens\in[0.3,0.9]$; per-model dense references from the same
measurement halves. Quantization and distillation arms are part of the
pre-registered grid (\S\ref{sec:planned}).
\textbf{Geometry.} $\gbar{c}$ and the streaming Fisher quadratic forms
of \S\ref{sec:geometry}, measured at each dense checkpoint; spectra
accumulated in $2\times10^{5}$ magnitude-quantile bins. Checkpoint
integrity is enforced by a hard guard (missing or re-initialized
weights abort the run), a protocol lesson learned the hard way.

Three regularities hold across every model measured:
(i) \textbf{capability ordering is family-invariant pre-cliff}: multi-hop
QA is the most pruning-robust of the three capabilities in all four
families, math and code the most fragile---capability selectivity is a
universal structure of scaling-down, not a family quirk;
(ii) \textbf{the cliff is universal and lives in a narrow proportional
band} ($\dens^{*}\in[0.35,0.7]$ across a $115\times$ range of model
sizes)---no model degrades gracefully through half density except
OLMo-3-7B, and none survives $\dens<0.3$;
(iii) \textbf{magnitudes and signs are family coefficients}: at matched
$\dens=0.7$, damage spans two orders of magnitude across families
(OLMo $+0.03$ to Gemma-3-27B $+4.0$), and the \emph{sign} of the QA
response separates Qwen (improves, up to $-0.79$) from all other
families (degrades). A scaling-down law must therefore have
family-invariant form with family-specific coefficients---the structure
our framework anticipates (\S\ref{sec:claims}).

\subsection{Mechanism: the signed first-order term}
\label{sec:mech}

The capability-geometry damage model \eqref{eq:taylor} makes its
sharpest prediction through the \emph{sign} of the first-order term
$\gbar{c}^{\top}\dw$, which no positive-semidefinite effective-parameter
model can express.

\paragraph{The QA sign is predicted, in both directions.}
In Qwen3, the QA alignment term is \emph{negative} (pruning small
weights moves QA downhill): at 4B it alone reproduces the measured
improvement, $-0.83$ predicted vs.\ $-0.79$ measured at $\dens=0.7$,
$-2.43$ vs.\ $-1.76$ at $\dens=0.6$. In Gemma-3, the QA alignment term
is \emph{positive} (e.g.\ 12B: $+0.26$ predicted vs.\ $+0.075$ measured
at $\dens=0.9$), predicting the \emph{absence} of any improvement---and
none is observed at any Gemma size. One measurable quantity, evaluated
at the dense checkpoint alone, anticipates on which family the
capability-selective free lunch exists. In OLMo-3-7B the mild-regime
prediction is sharpest of all (code: $+0.015$ predicted $=$ measured at
$\dens=0.8$).

\paragraph{The perturbative regime ends at the cliff.}
In every family, first- plus second-order predictions track measured
damage in the mild regime and then fail by orders of magnitude at the
cliff (e.g.\ Qwen3-0.6B math at $\dens=0.5$: predicted $-0.19$,
measured $+4.68$): the collapse is non-perturbative everywhere, in loss
space itself. This confirms the two-regime structure
\eqref{eq:tworegime} as a universal property of scaling-down, and
locates the scientific frontier at predicting $\dens^{*}_c$.

\subsection{Scale and family structure}
\label{sec:family}

\paragraph{Within-family robustness is non-monotonic in $N$---in two
families independently.} Gemma-3 robustness peaks at 12B: 27B collapses
far earlier ($+4.0$ vs.\ $+0.68$ at $\dens=0.7$) despite six years'
scale advantage. This mirrors, in an independent family and a different
measurement space, the prior Qwen-3 observation (ability space,
\S\ref{sec:reanalysis}) that 14B is the most prunable-robust source and
32B regresses. A mid-scale robustness peak is thus a reproducible
pattern of scaling-down, not an artifact of one family.

\paragraph{A candidate predictor, honestly retired.} We pre-registered
the top-concentration statistic $\kappa_c$ (\S\ref{sec:regimes}) as a
robustness predictor after it tracked the Qwen3 0.6B$\to$4B trend.
The full grid falsifies it as a portable predictor: across families the
correlation inverts (OLMo-3-7B is the most robust model with the
\emph{lowest} concentration, 2--10\%), and within Gemma-3 the top end
breaks it (27B has higher $\kappa$ than 12B yet is far more fragile).
$\kappa$ remains a within-ladder covariate below the robustness peak;
the family-level factor---plausibly related to over-training exposure,
by analogy with precision-fragility results
\citep{kumar2024scaling}---is open and testable via tokens-per-parameter.

\subsection{Cross-family panel at matched size ($\sim$30B)}
\label{sec:panel}

At matched $\sim$30B scale the family coefficient dominates:
$\Delta\Lc{\text{math}}$ at $\dens=0.7$ is $+0.17$ (Muse-30B), $+0.68$
(Gemma4-31B), $+4.04$ (Gemma3-27B), with OLMo-3-32B in progress and
Qwen3-32B available only in prior ability-space data. The panel turns
``which family should an agent deployment scale down from?'' into a
measured quantity---one axis of the method-selection map.

\subsection{Re-analysis of the prior ability-space grid}
\label{sec:reanalysis}

A prior internal study fit compression laws directly on Rasch abilities
from $\sim$100-item panels over a 774-cell Qwen3 grid and reported that
no derived law had held-out predictive power. Our re-analysis attributes
much of this to the measurement and testing layer:
(1) the pre-registered $r{=}1$ self-distillation falsification was
driven entirely by the answer-only recipe (math: $-3.22$ ability units
alone vs.\ $-0.13$ pooled without it); stratified, the anchor holds and
held-out transfer error drops from $9.0\times$ to $1.8\times$ the noise
floor; (2) same-benchmark evaluation inflated QA distillation gains by
roughly one third; (3) item discrimination heterogeneity invalidates
interval-scale ability (2PL preferred, $\Delta$AIC $196$--$348$) while
rankings survive ($\rho\ge0.98$); (4) item-level damage is smooth
pre-cliff (uniform-shift beats discrete-deletion mixtures), ruling out
skill-quantum deletion as an item-level mechanism. These findings fix
the design of \S\ref{sec:prelim}: continuous capability-conditional
loss as the primary observable, cross-benchmark probes, rank-only use
of latent abilities.

\subsection{Pre-registered grid in progress}
\label{sec:planned}

The remaining grid crosses the same capability panel with:
round-to-nearest quantization (int8/int4); single-teacher black-box
distillation onto Gemma-3 students (teachers: two frontier APIs,
recorded per-teacher and analyzed pairwise); and recovery training
$\Dr\in\{0,10\text{M},100\text{M},1\text{B}\}$ tokens. Analysis follows
\S\ref{sec:claims}: forms committed before fitting; keep/reject by
cell-level paired bootstrap; measurement-error-weighted fits; repeated
held-out extrapolation on the largest size, deepest compression, and
largest recovery budget; positive/negative controls.
