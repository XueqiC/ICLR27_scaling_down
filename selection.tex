\section{From Laws to Capability-Specific Selection}
\label{sec:selection}


The laws of \S\ref{sec:laws} are meant to be used: given an initial
model, a capability, and a resource constraint, which method and
setting preserves that capability best? We write the decision as
\begin{equation}
(m^{*},\boldsymbol\theta_m^{*})=\arg\min_{m,\;\boldsymbol\theta_m\in\mathcal F_m(B)}\;
\widehat L_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_m(B)$ is the set of settings of method $m$ that meet
the budget $B$. Methods are compared through their predicted
capability loss and their resource cost, not through their internal
equations, and the map for one capability need not coincide with the
map for another.

We validated this use on the controlled panel with a nominal storage
budget, using only measured configurations (84 pruning, 70
per-channel and 57 grouped quantization, and 9 same-stage smaller
students across 17 states) and refitting every law with the target
state held out, so that no prediction sees the state it selects for.
Over 289 state-budget cells per capability, the choice made from the
laws is within 0.19 (math), 0.17 (code), and 0.42 (QA) nats of the
measured oracle on average, and within 0.03 nats when the objective is
the worst of the three capabilities; it picks the oracle's method in
96\%, 95\%, 73\%, and 94\% of cells. The cheapest feasible configuration
loses 4.3 to 5.2 nats to the oracle. A fixed quantization-only policy
is as good as the map on math and code, because on this storage axis
quantization dominates those capabilities for most states
(Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:selection}); the map departs from it where the laws
predict otherwise, for QA on the 410M and 1.4B states that have a
smaller student, where distillation is chosen, and on the 1B and 6.9B
states whose QA improves under pruning, where pruning is chosen. The
fixed pruning-only and distillation-only policies cannot meet the
budget in 44\% and 63\% of cells. About half the cells are ``no clear
winner'' cells, in which the predicted gap between the two best methods
is smaller than the laws' held-out error; reporting the candidate set
there is the honest output, and the oracle's method lies in that set in
every such cell (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:selection_maps}). The same
pattern appeared earlier in an offline replay on twelve heterogeneous
models (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_cross}). These maps inherit the laws'
boundaries: they are validated on in-range states with a nominal
storage cost, without latency or the cost of recovery training, and
their QA cells are the least reliable.
