\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

\paragraph{Pruning densities.} On the three checkpoints that entered
no fit, at densities 0.85, 0.675, and 0.575, the delivered power form
has MAE 0.24, 0.24, and 0.68 for math, code, and QA: it beats the
strength-only curve (0.45, 0.49, 0.58) and zero change on every
checkpoint, has the lowest math error of all predictors, ties the
median curve on code, and loses QA to every source-free curve (median
0.22) because it over-predicts the QA improvement, so the median has
the lowest aggregate error (0.24 against 0.39). At $d=0.55$, beyond
the range, every source-conditioned form fails on every source (0.6
to 4.5 nats): the transfer range is the interpolation regime.

\paragraph{Quantization settings.} The per-channel predictor's one
unseen setting, 5 bits by a fixed interpolation rule, is worse than a
per-bit constant. The surface of Eq.~\eqref{eq:quant2d}, frozen on the two-bit-level
set, beat zero change at $b=4$ and $g=128$ on the development states
(0.19, 0.21, 0.44 against 0.45, 0.53, 0.48 at $b=4$), which
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_forms} traces to the regularized parametrization. In the confirmation round, with
predictions frozen after development on all 54 cells, group sizes 32
and 512 on two development states are predicted best by the
same-input piecewise interpolation (0.07, 0.12, 0.48 for math, code,
QA against 0.22, 0.31, 1.23 for the surface and 0.50, 0.56, 0.46 for
the median), and on a state never used in a quantization fit
(1.4B@112k) the source-free median is best or tied on every
capability (0.13, 0.22, 0.13 at the new group sizes; 0.006, 0.03,
0.16 at seen configurations), so source conditioning adds nothing
there (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}).

\paragraph{Distillation pools.} Under the uniform protocol the headline joint form is compared with the frozen
constant cell by cell, with paired differences and 95\% intervals
from a bootstrap over trajectories (Table~\ref{tab:distill_paired}). On the 270M
student at the unseen pool the gain over the constant is detected on
every capability (0.015, 0.011, and 0.26 nats; intervals above zero);
on the 1B student it is detected for code and QA (0.040 and 0.56
nats) and the math point estimate (0.007) is not resolved by three
trajectories. Against zero change the gains are 42 to 79\% on every cell. In a second confirmation (forms re-selected on all 25 trajectories
by the pre-committed rule, six new pools of $U{=}200$ at budgets 50k,
100k, and 200k, predictions frozen before training) every selected
form beats zero change, the gain over the strongest development-fixed baseline is detected
for code on both students (the one-parameter form $a_c\log(1+E)$,
which vanishes at zero budget, against the two-parameter
$c_c+b_c\log(1+E)$) and for QA on 270M,
and for math a budget-only or surface baseline is better on both
students (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}). The pool direction is
covered for code and QA; the selected math form adds nothing over
simpler frozen forms.

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

Inside the range, the frozen per-density pruning regression beat its
no-$D_0$ variant and the per-setting median on all three capabilities
at the unseen stage 96k, and for a 1B source at steps 32k and 112k
the power form, A2, and the median curve are within 0.05 nats of one
another: a new in-range size is predicted about as well by the source
relation as by the development median. For a 6.9B source, five times the development range, the
source-conditioned pruning forms lose to the median curve on math
(0.30 against 0.12) and QA (0.91 against 0.12) and tie on code: the
regression extends the panel's size trend into QA improvements that
do not occur, and the per-bit quantization regression is worse than
the per-bit median at every bit-width. On 6.9B@80k the power form ties the median on math and loses on
code and QA, and on a 2.8B source inside the refit size range the frozen median
density curve $\widetilde f_c(d)$ predicts within 0.08, 0.05, and 0.07
nats while both source-conditioned forms are three to
seven times worse (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}): on this new-size
panel the aggregated development response is the more accurate
predictor, and the extra value of $N_0$ and $D_0$ is not yet stable
enough to support the source-conditioned forms. For distillation, on Gemma-3-4B as a held-out student (6.6 times the
reference size) the frozen constant is better than the headline form
at the unseen pool on every capability (intervals below zero); at the development pools the headline form
has the lower point error on every capability and only its QA gain
clears zero (0.83 nats, interval $[0.12,1.54]$;
Table~\ref{tab:distill_paired}). Joint transfer to a larger student and a
new pool remains unreliable.

\subsection{Applicability across Capabilities and Shared Structure}
\label{sec:capabilities}

Math and code are carried by the initial-state inputs on every axis,
QA is not: its pruning response is over-predicted by every
source-conditioned form on every new checkpoint, and its quantization
error is the largest of the three on every test. What capability conditioning adds is measurable on the frozen
confirmation panels (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}): per-capability
pruning curves beat a shared damage curve with a per-capability scale
by 0.07 nats macro, almost entirely through QA, whose response has
the opposite sign (0.19 nats, interval $[0.13,0.25]$), while for math
and code a shared curve is as good or better; for grouped
quantization the two are indistinguishable (0.003 nats); and for
distillation the one-coefficient forms are algebraically equivalent,
with a shared curve plus per-capability offset predicting QA better
than the per-capability reuse form. Conditioning is needed where the
sign of the response differs across capabilities, and adds little
where the shape is shared.

\paragraph{Shared structure and ranges.} One function family across
methods holds for pruning by construction and fails for quantization
and distillation; within pruning one exponent shared across
capabilities costs nothing, whereas the quantization coefficients must
be capability-specific; parameter intervals, per-source exponent
spreads, and 80\% prediction intervals that under-cover (78\% and
67\%) are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}, together with
the one-point calibration result.
