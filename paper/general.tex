\subsection{Prediction at Unseen Compression Settings}
\label{sec:unseen_settings}

\paragraph{Pruning densities.} On three checkpoints that entered no fit (new stages of seen sizes, which the delivered rule assigns to the median curve), at densities 0.85, 0.675, and 0.575, the frozen power candidate has MAE
0.243, 0.236, and 0.68 for math, code, and QA against 0.230, 0.222,
and 0.68 for A2 and 0.277, 0.214, and 0.22 for the median curve: it
reaches A2's math error with a quarter of the parameters, trails the
median on code, and loses QA to every source-free curve. At $d=0.55$,
an extrapolation for the nine-state freeze, every source-conditioned
form of that freeze fails (0.6 to 4.5 nats).

\paragraph{Quantization settings.} The per-channel predictor's one
unseen setting, 5 bits by a fixed interpolation rule, is worse than a
per-bit constant. In the grouped confirmation, with predictions frozen
after development on all 54 cells, group sizes 32 and 512 on two development states are predicted best by the piecewise interpolation for math and code (0.07, 0.12 against 0.22, 0.31 for the frozen surface and 0.50, 0.56 for the median), while for QA the frozen zero and the median (0.46) edge the interpolation (0.48); on a state never used
in a quantization fit (1.4B@112k) the median is best for math and QA
at the new group sizes (0.13, 0.13), the interpolation is better for
code (0.17 against 0.22), and at seen configurations the median leads
on math and code while zero change is better for QA (0.05 against 0.16), and over the nine new-state cells the median's QA error is 0.14 against 0.22 for zero change: source conditioning adds nothing on that state.

\paragraph{Distillation pools.} On the first frozen test (nine
trajectories at an unseen pool) the headline joint form beats the
frozen constant on five of six development-student cells, with paired
intervals above zero (Table~\ref{tab:distill_paired}). In the
confirmation at six new pools of $U{=}200$ and three new budgets,
every selected form beats zero change; the gain over the development-designated baseline is detected for code on both students (0.004 and 0.005 nats) and for QA on 270M (0.09), for math a budget-only or surface baseline is better on both students, and the hindsight best of the sixteen frozen forms is a source-conditioned variant for every capability (Table~\ref{tab:main_final}).

\subsection{Transfer across Model Sizes and Training Stages}
\label{sec:unseen_states}

Inside the range, the frozen per-density pruning regression beat its
no-$D_0$ variant and the per-setting median at the unseen stage 96k,
and for a 1B source at steps 32k and 112k the power form, A2, and the
median curve are within 0.05 nats of one another. For a 6.9B source, five times the development range, the
source-conditioned pruning forms lose to the median curve on math
(0.30 against 0.12) and QA (0.91 against 0.12) and tie on code, and
the per-bit quantization regression is worse than the per-bit median
at every bit-width. On 6.9B@80k the power form ties the median on math and loses on code
and QA, and on a 2.8B source inside the refit size range the frozen
median density curve $\widetilde f_c(d)$ predicts within 0.08, 0.05,
and 0.07 nats while both source-conditioned forms are three to seven
times worse (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}): the aggregated
development response is the more accurate predictor there, and the
extra value of $N_0$ and $D_0$ is not yet stable enough to support the
source-conditioned forms. For distillation, on Gemma-3-4B as a held-out student (6.6 times the
reference size) the frozen constant is better than the headline form
at the unseen pool on every capability (intervals below zero); at the development pools only its QA gain clears zero (0.83,
$[0.12,1.54]$; Table~\ref{tab:distill_paired}).

\subsection{Applicability across Capabilities and Shared Structure}
\label{sec:capabilities}

Where the initial-state inputs help is method- and range-specific:
they carry math and code at unseen pruning densities of seen sizes
and at unseen group sizes of seen states, add nothing on new
quantization states or in distillation beyond the reference anchor,
and over-predict the QA pruning response on every new checkpoint. What capability conditioning adds is measurable on the frozen
confirmation panels (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}): per-capability
pruning curves beat a shared damage curve with a signed
per-capability scale by 0.06 nats macro (interval $[0.02,0.10]$ with
the 2.8B duplicate merged), almost entirely through QA (0.17,
$[0.11,0.24]$), whose aggregate curve is not proportional to the tested shared curve:
it changes sign across density (damage at $d=0.55$, improvement from
0.6 to 0.9), so no scale of that curve, including a negative one,
represents it ($R^2$ 0.49 against 0.94 and 0.97 for math and code),
and its direction also varies across sources. For math and
code a shared curve is as good or better, for grouped quantization
the two are indistinguishable (0.003 nats), and for distillation the
comparison is empty because the one-coefficient forms are
algebraically equivalent. Conditioning matters where the shape of the
response differs across capabilities.

\paragraph{Shared structure and ranges.} One function family across
methods holds for pruning by construction and fails for quantization
and distillation; within pruning one exponent shared across
capabilities costs nothing, whereas quantization coefficients must be
capability-specific; parameter intervals and 80\% prediction
intervals that under-cover (78\% and 67\%) are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}.
