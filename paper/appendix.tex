\subsection{Probes and loss computation}
\label{sec:app_probes}

The primary probes draw from MATH-500 \citep{hendrycks2021math,lightman2023verify}, MBPP
\citep{austin2021program}, and 2WikiMultihopQA \citep{ho2020constructing}; the secondary benchmarks of the measurement
checks are GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, SVAMP \citep{patel2021svamp}, HumanEval \citep{chen2021codex}, HotpotQA
\citep{yang2018hotpotqa}, TriviaQA \citep{joshi2017triviaqa}, and MuSiQue \citep{trivedi2022musique}. Models: Pythia
\citep{biderman2023pythia}, Gemma 3 \citep{gemma2025gemma3}, Qwen3 \citep{qwen2025qwen3}; LoRA adapters \citep{hu2022lora}.
The size-and-stage panel design follows the observational use of released checkpoints \citep{ruan2024observational,polo2024tinybenchmarks}.
Each capability is measured on a fixed probe set of prompt--reference
pairs drawn once, with a fixed seed, from held-out benchmark splits that
are disjoint from each training and recovery source used in the paper.
The primary measurement benchmarks are MATH-500 for mathematical
reasoning, MBPP for code generation, and 2WikiMultihopQA for multi-hop
question answering. For each capability we draw 128 probes and split
them by index parity into an estimation half, used only for gradient
and Fisher statistics, and a measurement half of 64 probes on which
each reported loss is computed, so that mechanism statistics and
evaluations do not share sampling noise. Secondary benchmarks used for the measurement checks below are
GSM8K, HumanEval, and HotpotQA on the pruning panel (no training
involved) and SVAMP, HumanEval, and TriviaQA where distilled models are
scored, chosen to exclude the benchmarks from which the distillation
traces were derived; a sample-level audit found no overlap between any
measurement probe and any training pool. The measurement half is 64
probes per capability for every result in the paper except where a
512-probe re-measurement is stated.


\subsection{Compression protocols}
\label{sec:app_compress}
\paragraph{Pruning.} Global magnitude pruning removes the smallest
weights across all transformer matrices (attention and MLP projections;
embeddings, output head, biases, and norms are excluded) to a target
retained density $\dens$. The global threshold is estimated from a
seeded sample of absolute weights so that the same procedure scales to
30B-parameter models; each density is applied to the same dense
reference weights, which are held on the CPU for the largest models so
that a single 80~GB device suffices.

\paragraph{Quantization.} Weights are fake-quantized per output channel
with symmetric round-to-nearest at bit-width $\bits$, using the integer
range $[-(2^{\bits-1}-1),\,2^{\bits-1}-1]$ and a per-channel scale set
by the channel's maximum absolute value; activations are left in
bfloat16. The dense weights are restored between bit-widths. The quantizer is
\texttt{fake\_quantize\_per\_output\_channel} in the released code:
$q_{\max}=2^{\bits-1}-1$ is the largest positive integer, integers are
clamped to $[-q_{\max},q_{\max}]$ ($2^{\bits}-1$ values), and the
scale is the channel's maximum absolute weight divided by $q_{\max}$.
With a fixed clipping range the step size from 4 to 5 bits shrinks by
$7/15$, so the squared step ratio is $(7/15)^2\approx0.22$; in general
the squared step ratio between adjacent bit-widths is
$(q_{\max}(\bits)/q_{\max}(\bits+1))^2$, not a constant $1/4$. A
squared step ratio becomes a loss ratio only under assumptions about
the error distribution and the local response that we do not test, so
the fixed $4^{-\bits}$ shape is one candidate among others rather than a
derived reference. Nominal storage ratios are computed from bit-widths;
no memory or latency was measured.

\paragraph{Distillation.} Students are fine-tuned on black-box teacher
traces (a frontier API teacher answering GSM8K, HotpotQA, and CodeAlpaca
prompts; 600 traces per domain) under the full-trace recipe, with
strict LoRA adapters of rank 16 and scaling 32 on the query, key, value,
output, gate, up, and down projections, AdamW at learning rate
$10^{-4}$, an effective batch of 16 sequences, and a cosine schedule
with 3\% warmup. A run aborts if the adapter library is unavailable and does not fall
back to full fine-tuning silently, and the actual
training mode and trainable parameter count are recorded in each
manifest. Token accounting distinguishes processed tokens (all input
tokens seen, including repetitions), unique pool tokens, and supervised
completion tokens. The data pool of $U$ examples per domain is either
the first $U$ rows of the trace file (the historical protocol) or a
random sample drawn with a pool-sampling seed that is independent of the
training seed; pools are identified by the sorted hashes of their
samples and pairwise overlaps are reported. The historical schedule
spanned a fixed number of epochs, so its cosine horizon varied with
pool size; the controlled matrix of \S\ref{sec:law_distill} uses a schedule
defined on the same absolute processed-token budget for each pool, and
the two protocols are kept apart in fitting.



\paragraph{Grouped quantizer.} The grouped variant (\S\ref{sec:law_quant}) splits each row of a weight matrix into
contiguous groups of $g$ input weights, pads the last group with zeros (which do not affect the group's absmax), and
applies the same symmetric round-to-nearest with $q_{\max}=2^{b-1}-1$ per group; $g$ equal to the row width reproduces
the per-channel quantizer bit-exactly (verified by a unit test). Configurations are $b\in\{3,4,5\}$ and
$g\in\{64,128,256\}$; nominal storage is computed from $b$ and $g$, and no memory or latency was measured.

\subsection{The controlled Pythia panel}
\label{sec:app_pythia}
The panel uses the public Pythia checkpoints at sizes 160M, 410M, and
1.4B and pretraining steps 16{,}000, 64{,}000, and 143{,}000, which
correspond to 33.6B, 134.2B, and 299.9B processed tokens at the

tests (Pythia-1B at steps 32{,}000, 96{,}000, and 112{,}000;
Pythia-6.9B at 32{,}000 and 112{,}000) were confirmed to have not
entered any fit or model selection before their predictions were
frozen.

\subsection{Grouped quantization: leave-one-state-out on six states}
\input{tables/v55_loso}

The low-order surface tested for grouped quantization is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta L}_c(\mathbf x,b,g)=\phi(\mathbf x)\cdot\big[1,\,u,\,v,\,uv,\,u^2\big],
\qquad u=\log_2 q_{\max}(b)\ \text{(centred)},\ v=\log_2\frac{g}{128},
\label{eq:quant2d}
\end{equation}
with one four-vector of state coefficients per term (twenty coefficients).

\paragraph{Identifiability of the two-dimensional form.} The
development bit-widths are $b\in\{3,5\}$, so the centred $u$ takes the
two values $\pm1.16$ and $u^2$ equals $1.35$ on every development
cell: the quadratic column is a multiple of the intercept column, the
design of Eq.~\ref{eq:quant2d} has rank 16 for its 20 coefficients,
and the ridge penalty resolves the four undetermined directions (the
effective degrees of freedom are 15.9 per capability;
Table~\ref{tab:quant_ident}). At the unseen $b=4$, where $u\approx0$
and $u^2\approx0$, the prediction depends on how the ridge split the
fitted intercept between the two columns. A same-input design without
the quadratic term spans the same development column space and gives
the same development fit, yet predicts the unseen bit-width far worse
(bit-test MAE 1.30, 1.39, 1.94 against 0.19, 0.21, 0.44), and so do
the bit-only and bit-plus-group additive designs; at $g=128$ all three
reduced designs coincide because the group terms vanish. The frozen
test result therefore stands as a result about the delivered
regularized parametrization, and no curvature in bit-width is
identified by the development data.

\paragraph{Shared structure, ranges, and calibration (moved from the main text).} One function
family for all three methods, with method- and capability-specific
parameters, holds for pruning by construction and fails for the other two arms on
the same held-out splits (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}). Within pruning one exponent shared across capabilities costs nothing
(leave-one-source MAE within 0.02 nats of per-capability exponents),
whereas the quantization coefficients must be capability-specific. Three ranges are kept apart
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}): the bootstrap intervals of the pruning coefficients exclude zero for
the dense-anchor term on math and code and not on QA; per-source
exponents spread widely (3.0 to 6.0 for math); and 80\% prediction
intervals from leave-one-source residuals
under-cover (78\% on the 27 confirmation-panel cells, 67\% on the 1B
and 6.9B pair cells) at a mean full width near 1.5 nats. One-point calibration of the pruning amplitude at the mildest density
amplifies noise and is no shortcut (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_shared}).

\section{Capability-Loss Measurement Checks}
\label{sec:app_measure}
\paragraph{Capability specificity as a prediction test.} On a
six-model, five-density pruning panel ($d$ from $0.9$ to $0.7$), the
response on the primary benchmark of each capability was used to
predict the response on the secondary benchmark of the same capability
through a two-parameter affine map fit out of fold, and compared with
the best cross-capability predictor chosen the same way. With whole
models held out, the same-capability gain is $+0.04$ nats for math
(95\% model-bootstrap interval $[0.03,0.05]$) and $+0.14$ for QA
($[0.02,0.27]$); with whole densities held out it is $+0.14$
($[0.03,0.30]$) and $+0.21$ ($[0.02,0.52]$). For code the
cross-capability predictor is as good or better ($-0.06$,
$[-0.16,-0.00]$ with densities held out), so code specificity is not
established at this resolution (Fig.~\ref{fig:measurement}). Six model clusters give descriptive
intervals only.



\paragraph{Same-capability agreement across the three operations.} On
Gemma-3-1B, six states fixed before the measurement (pruning at
$d=0.7$, RTN int4, and four distillation checkpoints of the uniform
protocol at $U=75$ and $U=450$) were scored on the main-protocol probe

term. A lower QA loss does not by itself imply better QA behavior: the

increase in exact match. This audit covers pruning only.

\paragraph{Reference conditions.} For a subset of models the
direct-answer and given-reasoning-answer conditional distributions were
measured in addition to the full-solution reference; they differ from

function body, so the numbers in this paper are specific to the

The loss is the conditional cross-entropy of the reference completion
given the prompt, summed over completion tokens and divided by the
number of completion tokens, as in Eq.~\ref{eq:taskloss}. Prompts and

1024 tokens; prompt tokens are masked out of the loss. All within-model
responses $\Delta L_c$ are differences in native-token nats
on identical token sequences. Because native-token units differ across
tokenizers, cross-family comparisons of absolute levels use per-byte
normalization or are reported separately, and no cross-tokenizer
quantity is treated as a common numerical scale. Negative responses are
retained as measured; nothing is clipped at zero.

\subsection{The pruning law}
\label{sec:law}

The mechanism form below fits the entire pre-cliff grid.
With the measured alignment term subtracted, the curvature residual
follows a power law in the deleted capability mass $m_c(\dens)$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Lc{c}(\dens)\;=\;\underbrace{\gbar{c}^{\top}\dw_\dens}_{\text{measured}}
\;+\;B\,\big[\underbrace{m_c(\dens)}_{\text{measured}}\big]^{\gamma},
\label{eq:fitlaw}
\end{equation}
with in-sample $R^2 = 0.82$--$0.996$ over 24 capability$\times$model
fits. The held-out battery (1000-resample cell bootstrap) is where the
form is tested: in \emph{leave-one-family-out} transfer the
shared-$\gamma$ mechanism form attains MAE $2.17$ nats versus $10.3$
for per-capability raw sparsity and $8.2$ for the direct-damage
baseline (both CIs exclude zero), so the deleted-mass coordinate is what
transfers across families; it also beats the direct-damage baseline in
fit-shallow/predict-deep ($3.11$ vs.\ $4.24$) and raw sparsity in
leave-largest-model-out ($1.41$ vs.\ $2.15$), while its edge over raw
sparsity within-model does not reach significance
(without the first-order term, QA is unfittable, $R^2\!\le\!0.67$;
with it, QA joins the law). Two structural results:
(i) in the deleted-mass coordinate the exponent concentrates at
$\gamma\in[1.2,2.2]$ across all families, versus $3.2$--$8.7$ in the
raw $(1-\dens)$ coordinate, which is what makes
the form portable; and (ii) \emph{within a model, $\gamma$ is shared
across capabilities} (e.g.\ Qwen3-0.6B: $1.41/1.56/1.43$;
Gemma3-4B: $1.66/1.71$): capability differences appear to be carried
by the two measured quantities, and the fitted structure is two
parameters per model. This mechanism form uses gradient
information of the dense model and is therefore not a K0 predictor; it
is reported as an upper comparator for the reduced forms of the main
text, not as a bound.

\subsection{The signed first-order term predicts, in both directions}
\label{sec:mech}

The alignment term $\gbar{c}^{\top}\dw$ is the family
coefficient that carries sign. Its sign predicts the presence or absence of the
capability-selective improvement in each case measured:
the QA term of Qwen3 is negative and QA improves under pruning (predicted
$-0.83$ vs.\ measured $-0.79$ at 4B, $\dens=0.7$); the QA term of Gemma is
positive and no improvement exists at any size; the term for OLMo-3-7B is
positive (mild degradation, prediction $+0.015$ $=$ measurement at
$\dens=0.8$, code) while the term for OLMo-3-32B is negative, and
deep-pruning QA improvement duly appears. At the level of individual model$\times$capability$\times$density
cells in the mild regime, sign prediction accuracy is $67.6\%$
(Wilson 95\% CI $[58.3,75.7]$, $n{=}108$), above chance, with the
family-level pattern (Qwen and OLMo-32B QA improvements, Gemma
none, an OLMo size flip) called in advance. Beyond the mild regime, first- plus second-order
predictions fail by orders of magnitude at the cliff in each family:
the collapse is non-perturbative in loss space, confirming the
two-regime structure of \eqref{eq:fitlaw}.

\subsection{The cliff is a geometric threshold crossing}
\label{sec:orderparam}

Measuring the capability geometry \emph{of the pruned models}
accounts for the location of the cliff on the three models tested. The
capability-region mass retention (the fraction of the
capability-exclusive top coordinates of the dense model still
prominent after pruning) declines smoothly from the first densities
(no cliff in geometry) while loss is still flat;
the behavioral cliff occurs where retention crosses a critical value.
The critical value is family-specific (Gemma: $0.035$--$0.10$ across
1B/4B and all capabilities; Qwen: $0.13$--$0.23$) but consistent
within a family across sizes and capabilities: the cliff boundary
$\dens_c^{*}(N_0,f)$ reduces to one critical-retention constant per
family, turning the least elegant fitted parameter of the two-regime
law into a measurable, smoothly evolving order parameter.
\preliminary{three models, two families; a multi-model extension is left to future work}

\subsection{Recovery arm: redeemable damage and the fitted plateau}
\label{sec:recovery}

Recovery training on general text fits the recovery law cleanly
(Gemma3-270M pruned to $\dens=0.6$; $R^2\approx1.0$):
$\beta'_{\text{math}}=1.23$, $\beta'_{\text{code}}=1.02$, with
fitted plateaus under the fixed LoRA recipe and the largest measured
budget of 16M tokens, $\Lc{c}^{\text{plateau}}-\Lc{c}^{\text{dense}}$,
of $1.21$ (math) and $1.93$ (code) nats; about $80\%$ of this pruning
damage is redeemed within 8M tokens and the remainder persists up to
the largest budget measured under this recipe, while QA recovers
completely. The plateau is a fitted quantity at 16M tokens, not an
established asymptote, and the saturating form does not describe the
non-monotone aligned-data trajectory below. \preliminary{one model, one density, one recipe; with three-point
ladders the largest-budget extrapolation of the saturating form beats a
carry-forward baseline in only 4 of 9 fits, so the ladder is too sparse
to claim extrapolation, and a denser multi-model grid is left to future work} Two mechanism results:
recovery on \emph{aligned} data (teacher traces) heals substantially
faster at small budgets (math $2.76$ vs.\ $3.47$ at $0.5$M tokens),
as the gradient-overlap prediction requires, but over-training on
narrow aligned data \emph{re-damages} the model (QA $4.95\to9.75$
from $0.5$M to $8$M tokens), while general-text recovery is monotone.
Aligned recovery is fast at small budgets and harmful when over-trained on narrow data; general recovery is slow and monotone on this model. Quantization damage past the bit cliff is markedly less redeemable
under the same recipe (residuals $\approx2.6$--$2.8$ after int3) than
pruning damage at matched severity.

\subsection{Re-analysis of an earlier ability-space grid}

A prior internal study fit compression laws directly on Rasch
abilities from $\sim$100-item panels over a 774-cell Qwen3 grid and
reported that no derived law had held-out predictive power. Our
re-analysis attributes much of this to the measurement layer:
(1) the $r{=}1$ falsification anchor was driven almost entirely by the
answer-only recipe (stratified, the anchor holds and held-out error
drops from $9.0\times$ to $1.8\times$ the noise floor); (2)
same-benchmark evaluation inflated QA gains by roughly one third;
(3) discrimination heterogeneity voids interval-scale ability while
rankings survive ($\rho\ge0.98$); (4) item-level damage is smooth
pre-cliff, ruling out discrete skill deletion at the item level.
These findings fixed the protocol of \S\ref{sec:loss}.

they read, and the main prediction table is produced by a single
command. Model measurements are reproducible from the recorded
revisions, probe seeds, and protocols above.
