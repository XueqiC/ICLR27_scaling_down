\subsection{Conditioning on the Initial Model State}
\label{sec:conditioning}

Is the initial state worth reading, and which part carries the
signal? On the nine-state panel with one stage held out, a per-setting
regression of the pruning response on $(N_0,D_0,L_{0,c})$ lowers the
error from 0.413, 0.516, and 0.500 nats to 0.253, 0.292, and 0.806
for math, code, and QA (interval above zero for code, crossing for
math, negative for QA). Adding the dense anchor $L_{0,c}$ to
$(N_0,D_0)$ has intervals above zero for math ($+0.55$, $[0.29,1.00]$)
and code ($+0.66$, $[0.38,1.13]$); adding $D_0$ to $(N_0,L_{0,c})$ does not (Fig.~\ref{fig:inputs}A). Every relation is therefore conditioned on the three inputs per
capability, with the reference anchor as the carrier.

\subsection{Pruning}
\label{sec:law_prune}

Pruning damage grows with the removed fraction $1-d$ with a
source-dependent amplitude, and QA can improve while math and code
degrade (Fig.~\ref{fig:responses}, left). The delivered form is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta L}_c(\mathbf x,d)=A_c(\mathbf x)\,\Big(\frac{1-d}{0.3}\Big)^{\gamma_c},
\qquad
A_c(\mathbf x)=\beta_c\cdot\big(1,\tilde{\log N_0},\tilde L_{0,c},\tilde{\log D_0}\big),
\label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
five parameters per capability, tildes standardized on the development
set (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_details}, Table~\ref{tab:models}). Its same-input alternatives are the per-density regression A2 (an
unseen $d$ by linear interpolation between fitted densities), the
power form with $\gamma_c=1$, and a two-term polynomial in $1-d$; the
source-free references are a strength-only curve, the median
development curve, and zero change. Fit on the nine states at $d\in\{0.9,0.8,0.7,0.6\}$ and evaluated at
the unseen 0.65 and 0.55, Eq.~\eqref{eq:power} cuts the strength-only
error by two to three times on every capability, and A2 matches it
(Table~\ref{tab:pred_main}): reading the source state is what
matters, and the power structure adds nothing over the per-density
regression. After the frozen tests of \S\ref{sec:general} both were refit on 17
states under a pre-committed leave-one-source-out rule: A2 (0.479) and
the power form (0.494) tie within 0.02 nats, and the rule prefers the
continuous form that vanishes at $d=1$. Its coefficients
are in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_coef}; $\gamma_c$ is a fitted constant
per capability whose value these data do not establish as necessary.

\subsection{Quantization}
\label{sec:law_quant}

Bit-width alone offers little room for a continuous relation: int8
and int6 stay within 0.01 nats of dense, int4 costs 0.03 to 0.35 nats,
and int3 collapses most models (Fig.~\ref{fig:responses}, middle). At fixed bit-width we use the
source regression of \S\ref{sec:conditioning} per bit; it predicts a
new state at a seen bit-width, is no law over $b$, and improves on the
per-bit median for math and code and not for QA. A grouped quantizer whose scale is shared by $g$ contiguous weights
adds a genuine configuration axis. On six development states at
$b\in\{3,5\}$ and $g\in\{64,256\}$ we fit, with the same inputs, a separable amplitude-times-shape
form, the low-order surface
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta L}_c(\mathbf x,b,g)=\phi(\mathbf x)\cdot\big[1,\,u,\,v,\,uv,\,u^2\big],
\qquad u=\log_2 q_{\max}(b)\ \text{(centred)},\ v=\log_2\frac{g}{128},
\label{eq:quant2d}
\end{equation}
with one four-vector of state coefficients per term, and a same-input
bilinear interpolation. With two development bit-widths $u^2$ is constant on the development
cells (rank 16 of 20, Appendix~\ref{sec:app_forms}), and with six
states the leave-one-state-out errors select no form. The frozen
tests of \S\ref{sec:unseen_settings} and a confirmation round with all
54 unblinded cells as development set (three bit levels, full rank)
and 21 frozen cells at unseen group sizes and on a new state settle
the choice: the delivered predictor for math and code is the
same-input piecewise interpolation over the measured $(b,g)$ grid with
a pre-specified linear boundary rule in $\log_2 g$, for QA and for a
new state the source-free per-configuration median, and the
parametric surface loses on every confirmation test.

\subsection{Distillation}
\label{sec:law_distill}

The response is the signed transfer $\delta_c$ of an existing student
to teacher traces relative to its own reference loss; the teacher
never enters it. Its coordinates are the supervised completion tokens
$T$, the pool size $D_U$, and the reuse count $E=T/D_U$. QA improves under every recipe we ran while math and code respond by
a few hundredths to a few tenths of a nat; with a small pool the QA
loss rises again at large $E$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:responses}, right). Distillation lowers the conditional loss on the 2Wiki probe
distribution and on a fresh sample of it, and on the same checkpoints
the loss rises on MuSiQue and TriviaQA
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_measure}); we therefore read the QA result as
a prediction relation on the measured 2Wiki distribution, repeatable
within it and not transferable in direction to other QA
distributions.

\paragraph{Source axis.} On the nine Pythia states as students, with
the teacher pool and budget fixed, a linear form
$\delta_c=\beta_c\cdot(1,\tilde{\log N_0},\tilde L_{0,c},\tilde{\log D_0})$
halves the error of a per-capability constant for math (0.014 against
0.031 with one stage held out), ties it for code (0.057 against
0.061), and is worse than a constant for QA (0.263 against 0.159):
the student state carries the math response.

\paragraph{Pool, budget, and student.} Under the uniform
absolute-exposure protocol (two development students, two pools,
three pool samples, four fixed checkpoints), eight forms per
capability in $T$, $E$, and a source term $k\log(N_S/N_{\mathrm{ref}})$
were frozen before any test run. The headline form, the joint form
$\widehat\delta_c=u\,(a_c+k_c n+b_c u+q_c v)$ with
$u=\log(1+T/T_{\mathrm{ref}})$, $v=\log(D_U/D_{\mathrm{ref}})$, and
$n=\log(N_S/N_{\mathrm{ref}})$, is the frozen form with the lowest
development error; that selection rule was stated after the tests, so
the candidates' predictions are prospective and the headline choice is
retrospective. On the nine test trajectories at an unseen pool its gain over the
frozen constant is detected on five of the six student-capability
cells of the two development students, and on the held-out 4B student
the constant is better at the unseen pool (\S\ref{sec:general}). A second, frozen confirmation at a new pool size and three new budgets
confirms the code relation and not the math one
(\S\ref{sec:unseen_settings}). Two forms that vanish at $T=0$ and carry a
student descriptor $z_c=\tilde L_{0,c}$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat\delta_c=(a_c+\lambda_c z_c)\log(1+E),\qquad
\widehat\delta_c=(a_c+\lambda_c z_c)\big(1-e^{-T/T_*}\big)+(b_c+\mu_c z_c)\log(1+E),
\label{eq:kdforms}
\end{equation}
were compared on the development matrix by leave-one-run-out
cross-validation and scored on the test matrix retrospectively
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_forms}): the second is the best of all forms
on the 270M unseen-pool trajectories, the first fails for QA because
it has no budget term, and neither beats the frozen constant for math
and code on the 4B student. The delivered relation is the joint form
inside the development size range; no joint student-pool-budget law
is claimed.
