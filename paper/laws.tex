Each arm delivers one relation in the interface of
Eq.~\ref{eq:interface}: relation, inputs and range, selection, and
boundary. The reference anchor $L_{0,c}$ is a measured quantity that
reconstructs an absolute loss; the response relation
$\widehat\Delta_{m,c}$ is the predictive claim, and a good absolute
prediction with a source-free $\widehat\Delta$ says nothing about
whether $N_0$ or $D_0$ predict the damage.

\subsection{Conditioning on the Initial Model State}
\label{sec:conditioning}

On the nine-state development panel with one stage held out, a
per-setting regression of the pruning response on $(N_0,D_0,L_{0,c})$
lowers the error from 0.413, 0.516, and 0.500 nats to 0.253, 0.292,
and 0.806 for math, code, and QA; adding $L_{0,c}$ to $(N_0,D_0)$ has
intervals above zero for math and code, adding $D_0$ does not
(Fig.~\ref{fig:inputs}A). The source-conditioned forms below use the
three inputs per capability, and every arm also carries a source-free
relation for the cases where the source inputs add nothing.

\subsection{Pruning}
\label{sec:law_prune}

\paragraph{Relation.} For a seen source state (size and stage in the fit) at an unseen density in the development range, the compact form
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta L}_c(\mathbf x,d)=A_c(\mathbf x)\,\Big(\frac{1-d}{0.3}\Big)^{\gamma_c},
\qquad
A_c(\mathbf x)=\beta_c\cdot\big(1,\tilde{\log N_0},\tilde L_{0,c},\tilde{\log D_0}\big),
\label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
with five parameters per capability (tildes standardized on the
development set) approximates the twenty-parameter per-density regression A2 for math and code; for any new state (a new size, or a new stage of a seen size, which takes precedence) and for QA everywhere, the delivered relation is the source-free median development curve,
$\widehat L_c=L_{0,c}+\widetilde f_c(d)$.
\paragraph{Inputs and range.} $N_0$, $D_0$, and $L_{0,c}$ for
Eq.~\ref{eq:power}; $L_{0,c}$ alone for the median curve. The
nine-state freeze covers $d\in[0.6,0.9]$ and $0.55$ was an
extrapolation for it; the 17-state refit used for the confirmations
includes 0.55.
\paragraph{Selection.} Fit on nine states at $d\in\{0.9,0.8,0.7,0.6\}$
and scored at the unseen 0.65 and 0.55, Eq.~\ref{eq:power} cuts the
strength-only error two- to threefold and A2 matches it; refit on 17
states under a pre-committed leave-one-source-out rule, A2 (0.479)
and the power form (0.494) tie within 0.02 nats and the rule prefers
the continuous form that vanishes at $d=1$ (coefficients in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_coef}; $\gamma_c$ is a fitted constant, not
established as necessary).
\paragraph{Boundary.} Evaluated as a frozen candidate on three new checkpoints, the power form reaches A2's math error with five parameters against twenty and does not beat the median curve on code or QA; on a 2.8B source the median curve is three to seven times better than either source form (\S\ref{sec:general}). The compact form trades accuracy for compactness within a tested range; the median curve is the relation for new states, a rule fixed after these tests and reused frozen in \S\ref{sec:selection}.

\subsection{Quantization}
\label{sec:law_quant}

\paragraph{Relation.} Per-channel RTN offers no continuous axis (int8
and int6 within 0.01 nats of the reference, int4 0.03 to 0.35, int3
collapse); at a seen bit-width the per-bit source regression of \S\ref{sec:conditioning} improved on the per-bit median for math and code only in the leave-one-state-out development evaluation, so a new state is predicted by the per-bit development median. Sharing one scale
across $g$ contiguous weights adds a second axis. The delivered grouped
relation for math and code on seen states is a same-input piecewise
interpolation: a per-configuration regression on
$(N_0,D_0,L_{0,c})$ at each measured $(b,g)$ cell, interpolated
linearly in $\log_2 g$ between measured group sizes at the same
bit-width and extrapolated linearly beyond the grid with a zero floor;
for QA, and for every capability on a new state, the delivered
relation is the per-configuration median of the development responses
under the same boundary rule.
\paragraph{Inputs and range.} The measured development grid
($b\in\{3,4,5\}$, $g\in\{64,128,256\}$ on six states) plus
$(N_0,D_0,L_{0,c})$ for the interpolation; $L_{0,c}$ alone for the
median.
\paragraph{Selection.} Two parametric candidates were tested first, a
separable amplitude-times-shape form and a low-order surface in
$\log_2 q_{\max}$ and $\log_2 g$; the surface beat zero change at the
unseen $b=4$ and $g=128$ on the two-bit-level development set, but its
quadratic term is unidentified there (rank 16 of 20,
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_forms}). In a confirmation round with all 54
unblinded cells as development set and 21 cells frozen at unseen
group sizes and on a new state, the interpolation won the frozen cells
for math and code and the median won the new state, so the delivered
rule was fixed after that round and is labeled retrospective
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_results}).
\paragraph{Boundary.} The interpolation holds at unseen group sizes of
seen states; on the new state the source-free median is as good or
better except for code, so source conditioning adds nothing there,
and the surface is not delivered (\S\ref{sec:unseen_settings}).

\subsection{Distillation}
\label{sec:law_distill}

\paragraph{Relation.} The response is the signed transfer $\delta_c$ of
an existing student relative to its own reference loss; the teacher
never enters it, and the coordinates are the supervised completion
tokens $T$, the pool size $D_U$, and the reuse count $E=T/D_U$. The
delivered relations, frozen for the confirmation, are
\begin{equation}
\widehat\delta_c=a_c\log(1+E)\ (\text{math, code}),\qquad \widehat\delta_{\mathrm{QA}}=u\,(a+b\,u+q\,v),
\label{eq:kdforms}
\end{equation}
with $u=\log(1+T/T_{\mathrm{ref}})$ and $v=\log(D_U/D_{\mathrm{ref}})$: one parameter for math and code, which vanish at zero budget, and three for QA; QA improves under every recipe we ran while math and
code move by hundredths to tenths of a nat.
\paragraph{Inputs and range.} $T$, $D_U$, and the student's reference
loss; no student descriptor. The relations hold on the two
development students (270M, 1B), and for QA on the 2Wiki distribution
only, where the gain replicates on a fresh sample and reverses sign on
MuSiQue and TriviaQA (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_measure}).
\paragraph{Selection.} Eight forms in $T$, $E$, and a source term were
frozen on twelve development runs and scored on nine unseen-pool
trajectories with the headline chosen after the test, and two
descriptor-carrying forms were compared retrospectively
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_forms}); the delivered forms were then
re-selected on all 25 trajectories by a pre-committed
leave-one-trajectory-out rule and frozen for six new pools of
$U{=}200$ at three new budgets. A linear form in the student state, fit on nine Pythia states, serves only as the math predictor for distillation candidates in \S\ref{sec:selection}.
\paragraph{Boundary.} At the new pool size the selected form beats the
frozen strongest baseline for code on both students, where that
baseline is the same $\log(1+E)$ form with an intercept, so the gain
supports the zero-budget boundary and parsimony of the form, not a
full student-data-training law; QA gains on 270M only, math loses to intercept baselines, no selected form is the hindsight best of the sixteen frozen forms (Table~\ref{tab:main_final}), and a 6.6-times-larger student is out of range.
