\begin{abstract}
Specialized workloads need specific capabilities, and the models that serve them are usually reference models scaled
down by pruning, quantization, or distillation; choosing a configuration today means
measuring each one. We systematically develop and test capability-conditioned relations
that predict the loss on math, code, and multi-hop QA after pruning,
quantization, or distillation from the initial model state (parameter
count, pretraining tokens, and the reference capability losses of the
source or initial student) and the configuration of the method,
separating configuration response, transfer to new model states, and
decision value. Relations are built on a controlled panel of Pythia checkpoints whose
size and pretraining stage vary independently and validated with
predictions frozen before new configurations and states are measured.
A five-parameter pruning form reaches, with fewer parameters, the
error of a per-density regression on three new checkpoints (0.24
against 0.23 nats for math) and does not beat a source-free median
curve on code or QA or on new sizes; with the group size as a second
axis, a frozen same-input interpolation over the measured grid predicts
unseen group sizes on the development states and a parametric surface
does not; under a fixed trace-distillation protocol a zero-at-zero reuse model beats its development-designated baseline for code on both development students and a joint budget and pool model does so for QA on one, neither is the best of the forms frozen alongside it in hindsight, and transfer to a larger student remains unreliable. We connect the relations to an explicit capability- and
budget-specific selection problem, validated retrospectively on
held-out states and, for one locked rule, prospectively on four fresh
states for math, code, and QA, and release coefficients, tested ranges,
registered predictions, and measurements.
\end{abstract}
