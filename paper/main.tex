\begin{abstract}
Specialized workloads need specific capabilities, and the models that
serve them are usually dense sources scaled down by pruning,
quantization, or distillation; choosing a configuration today means
measuring each one. We develop capability-conditioned empirical models
of the compression response: for math, code, and multi-hop QA we
predict the conditional loss after compression from the initial model
state (parameter count, pretraining tokens, and the dense capability
losses of the source or initial student) and the configuration of the
method. Relations are built on a controlled panel of Pythia checkpoints whose
size and pretraining stage vary independently and validated with
predictions frozen before new configurations and states are measured.
A five-parameter pruning form predicts math and code at unseen
densities inside the fitted range on three new checkpoints (0.24 nats
mean absolute error against 0.45 for a source-free strength curve) and
fails beyond the range and for QA; with the group size as a second
axis, a frozen same-input interpolation over the measured grid predicts
unseen group sizes on the development states and a parametric surface
does not; under a fixed distillation protocol a
response model in training budget, reuse, and a student descriptor
predicts unseen data pools on the development students, while joint
transfer to a larger student and a new pool remains unreliable. We connect the relations to an explicit capability- and
budget-specific selection problem, validated retrospectively on
held-out states and, for one locked rule, prospectively on four fresh
states for math, code, and QA, and release coefficients, tested ranges,
registered predictions, and measurements.
\end{abstract}
