\section{Predictive Scaling-Down Framework}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Dense reference candidates}
\label{sec:denseref}

The dense reference is a capability-conditioned specialization and
validation of established scaling laws \citep{kaplan2020scaling,
hoffmann2022training,gadre2024language}, not a contribution of this
paper. We compare, by held-out prediction error:
D1, size-only $E_c+A_cN^{-\alpha_c}$;
D2, Chinchilla-style $E_c+A_cN^{-\alpha_c}+B_cD^{-\beta_c}$;
D3, family-conditioned D2 with offsets $u_{c,f}$; and
D4, the observed dense capability anchor $\mathbf L_0^{\mathrm{obs}}$
used directly per \eqref{eq:anchored}. D2/D3 are claimed only where
$N$, $D$, and $f$ vary independently enough to identify them;
$D$--family collinearity is reported rather than hidden.
\resultpending{D1--D4 comparison; controlled $(N,D)$ grid}
The practical setting in this paper is D4: anchored prediction of
compression-induced change.

\subsection{Shared-plus-method-specific law hierarchy}
\label{sec:claims}

We do not presume that methods unify. The general form is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf L}^{(m)}
=\mathbf L_0^{\mathrm{obs}}
+F_{\mathrm{shared}}(\mathbf x_0,\mathbf z_m)
+F_m(\mathbf x_0,\mathbf z_m,\boldsymbol\theta_m),
\label{eq:hierarchy}
\end{equation}
and three hypotheses are compared by held-out prediction error (never
by in-sample $R^2$):
\textbf{M1}, fully shared: after transformation to common coordinates
$\mathbf z_m$, one response function covers all methods;
\textbf{M2}, hierarchical: shared macro-structure with capability,
family, and method corrections;
\textbf{M3}, method-specific functional forms unified only by the
capability-vector output, source interface, validation protocol,
uncertainty reporting, cost model, and agent-performance link.
\resultpending{M1/M2/M3 held-out comparison, including
leave-one-method-out and matched-storage / matched-active-parameter /
matched-initial-damage tests}
If unification fails at M1/M2, the M3 laws are reported separately,
and their use in a method-selection map is left to discussion
(\S\ref{sec:map}). The cross-method tests completed in this paper cover
the resource-ratio models at matched storage and active-parameter
coordinates; the full M1/M2/M3 held-out comparison is a stated
limitation (\S\ref{sec:planned}).

\subsection{Candidate functional forms per arm}
\label{sec:laws}

\paragraph{Pruning (two regimes).} Pre-cliff candidates, fit on the
positive-damage branch and compared by the held-out battery
(fit-shallow-predict-deep, leave-largest-model-out,
leave-one-family-out, sign prediction, cliff-density prediction;
baselines: raw sparsity, remaining parameters, removed weight norm,
anchor-only, size-plus-density, family-conditioned reduced form):
simple reduced forms in $(\dens; N_0, f)$, and, as mechanism
comparators only, the measured-alignment-plus-deleted-mass form
$\Delta\Lc{c}=a_c(\dens)+B\,m_c(\dens)^{\gamma}$ whose in-sample fits
motivated this study (\S\ref{sec:law}). Cliff modeling compares a hard
threshold against a smooth sigmoid in $\dens$ and, following the
geometric order-parameter evidence (\S\ref{sec:orderparam}), a
family-level critical-retention parameterization of
$\dens^{*}_c(N_0,f)$. \resultpending{held-out battery results}

\paragraph{Quantization.} Candidates at bit-width $\bits$: $q_c4^{-\bits}$,
a learned exponential $q_ce^{-k\bits}$, polynomial degradation, and an
effective-parameter multiplier; validated by leave-one-bit-out and
held-out model/family, with a matched-storage comparison against
pruning. \resultpending{functional-form comparison}

\paragraph{Distillation.} Per capability, a template with student
capacity floor, teacher capability vector $\mathbf L^{\mathcal T}$,
a data power law in trace tokens, and a supervision-dependent coverage
factor $\chi_c(\rho)$ computed from data composition (never inferred
post hoc from results); capacity-gap corrections follow
\citet{busbridge2025distillation}. Confounds (total tokens, update
count, answer format, teacher identity) are controlled by
token-matched and update-matched runs. \resultpending{full
capacity$\times$teacher$\times$data$\times$recipe grid}

\paragraph{Recovery.} The saturating transform, anchored at
$\Dr{=}0$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\hat L_c^{(m)}(\Dr)=\Delta L_c^{(m)}(0)\,
\Big[r_{m,c}+(1-r_{m,c})\big(1+\Dr/D_{0,m,c}\big)^{-\beta_{m,c}}\Big],
\label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
where $r_{m,c}$ is the \emph{asymptotic residual fraction under the
fixed recovery recipe}, not a claim of fundamentally unrecoverable
capability. Recovery-source dependence (general vs.\
capability-aligned vs.\ mixed data) is an explicit axis.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} Configuration-level cluster bootstrap for
fitted coefficients; benchmark bootstrap for deterministic
compression; multiple seeds for training-based arms at small scale;
calibration coverage reported alongside MAE and relative error.

\subsection{Mechanism analysis: capability geometry}
\label{sec:geometry}

As supporting evidence rather than a prediction interface, we measure the
capability-conditional mean gradient $\gbar{c}$ and Fisher structure
$\Fc{c}=\mathbb{E}_{i\sim c}[g_ig_i^{\top}]$ at the dense checkpoint,
streamable at 30B scale (diagonal spectra in magnitude-quantile bins;
quadratic forms via $\dw^{\top}\Fc{c}\dw=\mathbb{E}_i[(g_i^{\top}\dw)^2]$).
Three uses: (i) testing whether capabilities occupy distinct,
partially separable parameter regions (\S\ref{sec:regions});
(ii) the \emph{signed} first-order term $\gbar{c}^{\top}\dw$, which no
positive-semidefinite effective-parameter model can express and which
anticipates capability-selective sign effects (\S\ref{sec:mech}); and
(iii) upper-bound comparators for reduced-form laws: if a reduced
form approaches the accuracy of the mechanism form without gradient
access, the practical interface loses nothing.

\subsection{Fitting and validation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Functional forms and keep/reject rules are committed before fits;
model comparison uses repeated held-out extrapolation (largest size,
deepest compression, largest recovery budget, leave-one-family-out),
never in-sample fit indices \citep{li2025misfitting,
besiroglu2024chinchilla}; manuscript counts of models, benchmarks, and
fits are generated from result files, not typed by hand; negative
results are reported, not tuned away.

\subsection{Discussion: toward a method-selection map}
\label{sec:map}

Intersecting per-method predictors at matched cost coordinates would
partition the (compression budget $\times$ capability requirement)
space into regions where pruning, quantization, distillation, or a
composition is cheapest, with uncertainty bands, and would be evaluated
against exhaustive search by constraint-satisfaction rate, cost regret,
selected-method accuracy, and the number of compression experiments
avoided. This is how the predictors of this paper would be used, not a
result of it: no held-out agent or method-selection validation was run
(\S\ref{sec:planned}), and the Pareto and mis-ranking analyses we
report are implications of prediction error rather than a validated
selector.
