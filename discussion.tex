\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

What can be predicted now is specific. Given the parameter count, the
pretraining token count, and the dense capability losses of a source
in the development range, the pruning form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:power}
predicts the math and code response at any density inside the fitted
range to within a few tenths of a nat, and a per-density regression
does the same without the power structure. Given the same inputs, the
frozen surface of Eq.~\eqref{eq:quant2d} predicts the grouped
quantizer at bit-widths and group sizes it was not fit on, for the
states it was fit on. Given a student's state, a linear form predicts
its math transfer; under a fixed distillation protocol, a response
model in budget, reuse, and a student descriptor predicts unseen data
pools on the two development students. Each comes with coefficients
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_coef}) and a tested range, and
\S\ref{sec:selection} connects them to an explicit selection problem.

What cannot be predicted is equally specific. No source-conditioned
form predicts the QA response to pruning on a new checkpoint; a
source-free curve is the better deliverable for that capability. No
form transfers to density 0.55 or to a source five times larger than
the development range, except that the pruning math error at the
larger source is on par with the source-free curve. Bit-width alone
supports no continuous quantization relation, the curvature of the
two-dimensional surface in bit-width is fixed by regularization, and
the separable candidate we tested is worse. Joint transfer of the
distillation forms to a larger student and a new pool is unreliable,
and distillation lowers the conditional loss on the primary QA
distribution without that change carrying over to TriviaQA, so the QA
result is a prediction relation on the measured distribution and no
general QA gain (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_measure}).

For further development these results say three things. The dense
capability anchor is the input to keep and training tokens add little
beyond it, so metadata-only prediction is not yet available. The forms
that work are low-dimensional and their exponents are not established
as necessary, so the next question is which inputs carry the QA
response. Transfer across sizes is bounded by the development range,
so a wider development panel is the direct route to wider laws. The
limitations are those of small controlled panels with correlated
observations (three sizes, three stages, three pools), of one
quantizer family and one teacher, of a loss endpoint whose code
specificity was not detected on a secondary benchmark, and of a
selection validation that uses nominal storage as the only cost.
