\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

What can be predicted now is specific. Given the parameter count, the
pretraining token count, and the dense capability losses of a source
in the development range, the pruning form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:power}
predicts the math and code response at any density inside the fitted
range to within a few tenths of a nat, and a per-density regression
does the same without the power structure. Given the same inputs, the
two-dimensional form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:quant2d} predicts the response
of the grouped quantizer at bit-widths and group sizes it was not fit
on, for the states it was fit on. Given a student's state, a linear
form predicts its math transfer; given a fixed student, a reuse-count
term predicts the QA transfer on a new pool with a bias that changes
sign across budgets. Each of these comes with coefficients
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_coef}) and a tested range, and together they
select methods and settings for held-out states within 0.2 nats of the
measured optimum for math and code under a storage budget
(\S\ref{sec:selection}).

What cannot be predicted is equally specific. No source-conditioned
form predicts the QA response to pruning on a new checkpoint; a
source-free curve is the better deliverable for that capability. No
form transfers to density 0.55 or to a source five times larger than
the development range, except that the pruning math error at the
larger source is on par with the source-free curve. Bit-width alone
supports no continuous quantization relation, and the separable
amplitude-times-shape candidate fails once group size is added. The
distillation forms frozen on two students and two pools transfer to an
unseen pool on both students; on a student 6.6 times the reference
size they beat the constant at the development pools and lose to it at
the unseen pool, and the QA response itself is specific to the probe
distribution
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_measure}).

For further development these results say three things. The dense
capability anchor is the input to keep and training tokens add little
beyond it, so cheaper metadata-only prediction is not yet available.
The functional forms that work are low-dimensional and the particular
exponents are not established as necessary, so the next question is
which inputs carry the QA response rather than which exponent to fix.
Transfer across sizes is bounded by the development range, so a wider
development panel is the direct route to wider laws. The limitations
are those of small controlled panels with correlated observations
(three sizes, three stages, three pools), of one quantizer family and
one teacher, of a loss endpoint whose code specificity was not
detected on a secondary benchmark, and of a selection validation that uses nominal storage as the only
cost, without latency, recovery-training cost, or any agent-level
measurement.
