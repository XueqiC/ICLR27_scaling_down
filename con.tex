\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether the model state observable before compression,
together with the intervention setting, predicts the
capability-conditioned loss after pruning, quantization, and
distillation, and over which ranges such predictions transfer. The
answer has three parts. Responses are heterogeneous across
capabilities and model series in sign and magnitude, so a
capability-conditioned endpoint is necessary and no single ordering
holds. Source-state information helps: the dense capability vector
carries the prediction, source-conditioned pruning predictors beat
source-free curves at seen densities and at interpolated densities on
the development sources, mostly for math and code, and match them at a
new in-range size; the power structure adds nothing over a per-density
regression, and training tokens add little beyond the dense anchor. The
transfer range is bounded: the same predictors fail at density $0.55$
and at a source five times larger, quantization predictors add value
only in the collapse regime and under some fitting protocols, unless
the group size is added as a second axis, where a low-order relation
predicts unseen configurations on seen states, and for
distillation a reuse-count coordinate helps QA on an unseen pool while
a constant is best for math and code. Method-agnostic resource-ratio
laws fail a matched-storage test. These results identify conditional
predictive relationships and their tested limits and provide the
measurements, registers, and comparison protocol on which
capability-conditioned scaling-down laws can be developed; widening
the size range of the development panel and finding inputs that carry
the QA response are the next steps, and both need new experiments.
