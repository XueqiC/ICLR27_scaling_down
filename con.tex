\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether the model state observable before compression,
together with the intervention setting, predicts the
capability-conditioned loss after pruning, quantization, and
distillation, and over which ranges such predictions transfer. The
answer has three parts. Capability responses are heterogeneous across
capabilities and model series, in sign as well as magnitude, so a
capability-conditioned endpoint is necessary and no single ordering
holds. Source-state information helps: the dense capability vector is
the input that carries prediction, and source-conditioned pruning
predictors beat source-free curves at seen densities and at
interpolated densities on the development sources, mostly for math and
code, and match them at a new in-range size;
the particular power structure adds nothing over a per-density
regression, and training tokens add little beyond the dense anchor. The
transfer range is bounded: the same predictors fail at density $0.55$
and at a source five times larger, quantization predictors add value
only in the collapse regime and only under some fitting protocols, and
for distillation a reuse-count coordinate helps QA on an unseen pool
while a constant is best for math and code. Method-agnostic
resource-ratio laws fail a matched-storage test. These results identify
conditional predictive relationships and their tested limits, and they
provide the measurements, registers, and comparison protocol on which
capability-conditioned scaling-down laws can be developed; extending
the size range of the development panel and finding inputs that carry
the QA response are the next steps, and both require new experiments.
