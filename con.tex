\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced scaling-down laws: capability-vector predictive laws for
LLM compression in the standard scaling-law format, organized by a
selective-deletion view of pruning, quantization, and distillation, and
grounded in a measurable mechanism object, the capability geometry of
the source model. Across four families and eleven models, the
evidence supports a specific law structure: form is family-invariant
(universal capability ordering; a universal, proportional,
non-perturbative collapse band; a perturbative regime governed by the
signed first-order geometry term), while magnitudes and signs are
family coefficients. The sharpest of these is the sign of the QA
alignment term, which predicts on which family pruning improves
retrieval-style capability. Two findings frame the road ahead: the mid-scale
robustness peak reproduced in two families, whose mechanism our
retired concentration predictor does not explain, and the collapse
boundary $\dens^{*}_c(N_0,f)$, which the completed
distillation-and-recovery grid will attack with the same pre-registered
discipline. The deliverables, reduced-form laws in basic parameters
and the method-selection map, aim to make ``what should this agent
scale down from, and how?'' a computed answer rather than a folklore
one.
