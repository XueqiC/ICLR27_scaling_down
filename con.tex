\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced scaling-down laws: capability-vector predictive laws for LLM
compression, organized by a selective-deletion view of pruning,
quantization, and distillation, and grounded in a measurable central
object---the source model's capability geometry. Preliminary evidence on
Qwen3 establishes that compression effects are capability-selective at
first order, that the signed alignment term of the geometry predicts the
perturbative regime quantitatively, that the collapse regime is
non-perturbative and must be treated as a separate law, and that a simple
concentration statistic of the geometry tracks prunability across scale.
The completed program delivers reduced-form laws in basic parameters and a
method-selection map for practitioners, together with an open-source
measurement suite. Beyond compression, capability geometry offers a
general lens on how heterogeneous abilities coexist in one network---and on
what, exactly, a small model must give up.
