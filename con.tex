\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We systematically investigated whether the model state observable
before compression, together with the intervention configuration, can
predict the capability-conditioned loss after pruning, quantization,
and distillation, testing source-state transfer and unseen-configuration
prediction separately with the per-capability conditional loss vector as
the common prediction endpoint. On a controlled panel, a
source-conditioned pruning predictor predicts unseen compression
densities with far lower error than strength-only baselines, and the
advantage carries to a new source of in-range size; at a source five
times larger it loses to a source-free median curve. A same-input
comparison shows no additional advantage for a shared power form. The predictive gain of the quantization candidate depends on the
precision regime, and damage-magnitude transfer under aggressive
quantization remains unstable. For distillation, the reuse-count coordinate improves QA-loss
prediction on an unseen data pool; for math and code it does no better
than a constant baseline, and it carries a budget-dependent bias. Across the heterogeneous panel, capability responses are
anisotropic with an invariant ordering and family-specific magnitudes
and signs, the capability geometry of the source model supports the
measurement layer as mechanism evidence, and laws that use only resource
ratios did not extrapolate across the methods tested. That rules out
those models and leaves open whether another single form could unify
the arms. These results delimit the information the current predictors
need, the range over which they transfer, and their limits, providing
tested candidate relations and an evaluation basis for developing
capability-conditioned scaling-down laws. How such predictors would
enter a capability- and cost-aware method-selection map is left to
future use (\S\ref{sec:map}); the controlled panels are small and their
intervals panel-conditional, and we state these limits explicitly.
