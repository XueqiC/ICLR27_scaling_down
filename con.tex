\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced capability-conditioned scaling-down laws: predict a
vector of capability losses after compression from a dense source state
observable before compression, common compression coordinates, and
method-specific controls, with pruning, quantization, and distillation
as parallel arms held to one evidence standard. Two axes carry the
evidence. On the source axis, a controlled Pythia panel shows that
source-state information, including training tokens, predicts the
compression response beyond dense loss for math and code but not QA,
and a frozen pruning predictor generalized to a never-fit training
stage. On the strength axis, source-conditioned inputs predict pruning
damage at unseen densities far better than any strength-only curve,
although a per-density fit ties the shared power form, so the inputs
rather than a particular exponent carry that result; quantization is
validated per configuration, with an unstable collapse magnitude at
three bits and no demonstrated gain in the near-lossless region; and
distillation shows capability-specific transfer in which the reuse
coordinate helps QA on an unseen pool while a constant suffices for
math and code, with a bias-dominated residual. Across the heterogeneous
panel, capability responses are anisotropic with an invariant ordering
and family-specific magnitudes and signs, the capability geometry of
the source model supports the measurement layer as mechanism evidence,
and capability loss remains a common outcome coordinate while
compression ratio does not serve as a common intervention coordinate.
Whether one response form unifies the arms remains open; the evidence
negates only the resource-ratio models we tested, so we unify at the
decision layer through a capability- and cost-aware method-selection
map, offered as a framework deliverable rather than a validated
selector. The controlled panels are small and their intervals
panel-conditional; these limits are stated rather than filled in.
