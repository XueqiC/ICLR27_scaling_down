\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Deployed language-model agents are specialists: a coding agent lives
on code synthesis, a retrieval assistant on multi-hop question
answering, and both pay for every token of inference. The model that
serves such a workload is usually a dense source scaled down by
pruning, quantization, or distillation, and choosing the route and its
strength today means running each candidate through capability
benchmarks. A single compression ratio cannot replace that search,
because compression does not shrink capabilities uniformly: on twelve
public models from five series, the same pruning density leaves QA the
least-damaged capability in Qwen3 and OLMo-3-32B and the most-damaged
one in the Gemma series, and 3-bit quantization collapses twelve of
fourteen models but not OLMo-3-32B (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_hetero}).

We therefore ask how the capability-conditioned loss after compression
can be predicted from the initial model state and the configuration of
the method. The endpoint is the conditional loss of each capability on
a fixed probe set, written relative to a reference measured
beforehand, the dense source for pruning and quantization and the
initial student for distillation (Eq.~\eqref{eq:interface}). The
admitted inputs are the parameter count, the pretraining token count
where disclosed, the dense capability losses of the reference, and the
intervention setting; no measurement of the compressed target enters a
prediction. The relations are developed on a controlled panel of
Pythia checkpoints whose size and pretraining stage vary
independently, by comparing low-dimensional forms that use the same
inputs, and tested by freezing coefficients and predictions before new
configurations and states are measured. This separates whether the
initial-state inputs help, whether a form adds anything over a
same-input alternative, and how far the relation transfers.

The outcome is a set of method-specific relations with measured
ranges. For pruning, a five-parameter power form with a
source-conditioned amplitude predicts math and code at unseen
densities inside the fitted range, ties a per-density regression, and
fails beyond the range and for QA. For quantization, bit-width alone
supports only a per-bit source regression, and with the group size as
a second axis a frozen low-order response surface predicts unseen
bit-widths and group sizes on the development states. For
distillation, under a fixed protocol a response model in training
budget, reuse count, and a student descriptor improves prediction for
unseen data pools on the two development students, while joint
transfer to a larger student and a new pool remains unreliable. Our
contributions are (1) the prediction problem, its admitted inputs, and
a development-then-validation design that separates input value, form
value, and transfer range; (2) the per-method relations, their
coefficients, and their independent tests against same-input and
source-free baselines, with the capabilities and settings they cover
and where they stop; and (3) a connection of these relations to an
explicit capability- and budget-specific selection problem, validated
retrospectively on held-out states, with measurements, registered
predictions, and code released.
