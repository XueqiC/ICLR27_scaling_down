\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Frontier language models are large and expensive because they are
general. Deployed agentic systems are the opposite: a coding agent, a
retrieval assistant, or a tool-calling workflow exercises a narrow,
stable \emph{combination} of capabilities, and its economics are
dominated by inference cost. The practical question of the small-model
era is therefore not ``how good can an $N$-parameter model be?'' but
``what is the cheapest model whose \emph{capability profile} covers
this workload?'' The standard route to such a model starts from a
strong source and scales it \emph{down}---by pruning, quantization, or
distillation, usually followed by recovery training.

Scaling up is governed by laws
\citep{kaplan2020scaling,hoffmann2022training} whose usefulness comes
from a strict format: few basic parameters, a continuous observable,
declared functional forms, validation by extrapolation. Scaling down
has no such laws. What exists are compression laws of
\emph{pretraining loss}---for sparsity \citep{frantar2023scaling},
precision \citep{kumar2024scaling}, distillation
\citep{busbridge2025distillation}, and unified compressed formats
\citep{panferov2025unified}---which are accurate in their space but
silent about the quantity practitioners buy small models for: which
capabilities survive. Meanwhile the empirical record shows compression
damage is strongly capability-selective \citep{jaiswal2023compressing,
liu2025quantization,yin2023junk}, and a single scalar loss cannot
express selectivity at all.

This paper develops \emph{scaling-down laws} in the standard format,
with two deliberate choices. First, the observable is a
\emph{vector} of capability-conditional losses
$\vec{L}=(\Lc{c})_{c\in\capset}$, continuous by construction
(\S\ref{sec:prelim}), because a decade of downstream-predictability
results shows accuracy-space fitting destroys the regularity laws need
\citep{schaeffer2024predicting,gadre2024language}. Second, functional
forms are \emph{derived from a mechanism, then validated by
extrapolation}, not selected by fit quality. The mechanism is the
\emph{selective-deletion} view: pruning deletes weight coordinates,
quantization deletes bit-planes, distillation deletes function
components the student cannot hold or the transfer data never
exercises; recovery training is the sole addition operator. What each
capability loses is governed by the source model's \emph{capability
geometry}---the capability-conditional mean gradient $\gbar{c}$ and
Fisher structure $\Fc{c}$ at the dense checkpoint, measurable with
forward--backward passes only (\S\ref{sec:method}).

Instantiating the laws on twelve dense checkpoints from four model
families (Qwen3, Gemma-3/4, Muse, OLMo-3; 270M--32B), we find that
scaling-down has law-like structure of a specific kind---%
\textbf{family-invariant form with family-specific coefficients}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Capability regions are real and causal.} Independent
benchmarks of the same skill share gradient signatures that
\emph{anti-align} across skills (block structure in seven models,
three families), and zeroing a capability's exclusive $0.05\%$ of
weight coordinates---selected from gradients alone---destroys exactly
that capability (near-diagonal ablation matrix; the off-target effect
of the QA ablation on code is $+0.0001$ nats). The capability vector
of our laws indexes measured objects, not benchmark conventions.
\item \textbf{The pre-cliff law is two measurements plus two
parameters.} $\Delta\Lc{c} = \gbar{c}^{\top}\dw + B\,m_c^{\gamma}$
fits 24 capability$\times$model curves at $R^2\ge0.82$ (mostly
$\ge0.95$); the exponent concentrates at $\gamma\in[1.2,2.2]$ in the
deleted-mass coordinate and is shared across capabilities within a
model. The \emph{sign} of the measured alignment term predicts on
which families pruning improves retrieval-style QA---four families
called correctly, including a sign flip \emph{within} OLMo-3 between
7B and 32B.
\item \textbf{The cliff is a geometric threshold crossing.} Collapse
is universal and non-perturbative in loss (no local expansion sees it
coming), but capability-region mass retention decays \emph{smoothly}
from the first densities, and the cliff sits where retention crosses
a family-specific critical value---reducing the two-regime law's
boundary to one measured constant per family.
\item \textbf{The other arms inherit the structure.} Quantization
shows the same capability selectivity in a different basis and a
universal cliff at $3$ bits; distillation confirms the pre-registered
coverage-deletion prediction (stripping chains-of-thought selectively
damages math); recovery training obeys a clean power law whose
unrecoverable residual ($\approx20\%$ of pruning damage) is the true
price of scaling-down---and aligned recovery data heals faster but
re-damages under over-training, a practical trade-off the
method-selection map encodes. Within-family robustness is
non-monotonic in size in two families independently; our
pre-registered spectral predictor for this is honestly retired.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) The scaling-down-law format: basic parameters, capability-vector
observable, and derived two-regime forms for pruning, quantization,
distillation, and recovery (\S\ref{sec:prelim}--\S\ref{sec:method});
(2) capability geometry as the measurable mechanism object, with the
signed first-order term as its sharpest validated prediction;
(3) a four-family, eleven-model empirical grid establishing the
invariances and family coefficients above, plus a forensic re-analysis
of a prior ability-space grid that fixes the measurement protocol
(\S\ref{sec:exp}); and (4) the \emph{method-selection map} as the
practitioner-facing deliverable (\S\ref{sec:map}), with the
distillation and recovery arms of the pre-registered grid in progress.
