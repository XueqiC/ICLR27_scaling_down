\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Capability-Conditioned Loss}
\label{sec:loss}

We measure three capabilities, mathematical reasoning, code generation,
and multi-hop question answering, each through a fixed probe set
$\probeset{c}$ of prompt and reference pairs drawn once from held-out
splits of MATH-500, MBPP, and 2WikiMultihopQA. The observable is the
token-weighted conditional cross-entropy of the reference completion,
\begin{equation}
\Lc{c}(w)\;=\;\frac{1}{T_c}\sum_{(x,y)\in\probeset{c}}\;\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}
-\log p_w\!\left(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}\right),\qquad
T_c=\sum_{(x,y)\in\probeset{c}}|y|,
\label{eq:taskloss}
\end{equation}
with prompt tokens masked, sequences truncated to 1024 tokens, the
model's own tokenizer, and the same 64 measurement probes per
capability for every state that is compared within a model. All
responses are within-model differences on identical token sequences,
in native nats per token: for pruning and quantization the damage
$\Delta L_c=L_c(\text{compressed})-L_c(\text{dense})$, and for
distillation the signed transfer
$\delta_c=L_c(S_{\mathrm{KD}})-L_c(S_0)$ of a student relative to its
own initial state. Three boundaries apply throughout. The observable
is the likelihood of a fixed reference, not a task-success measure; a
QA loss can fall while exact-match accuracy does not rise. Native units
differ across tokenizers, so absolute levels of different families are
never placed on one scale. Capability specificity is a tested
property rather than an assumption: on a pruning panel, the response
on an independent secondary benchmark is predicted better from the
same capability's primary response than from another capability's
for math and QA, whereas for code no within-capability gain was
detected at the available resolution (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_measure}).

\subsection{Models and Compression Settings}
\label{sec:models}

Three kinds of model panel serve three roles (Table~\ref{tab:panels}).
A \emph{heterogeneous panel} of finished public models establishes
the phenomena and the limits of cross-family transfer. A
\emph{controlled panel} of Pythia checkpoints varies the initial model
state along known axes, parameter count and pretraining stage, with
architecture and data fixed; it is the development set of every
relation in \S\ref{sec:laws}. \emph{New states and configurations}
that entered no fit provide the validation of \S\ref{sec:general}.

The three operations are held fixed by protocol. Pruning is global
magnitude pruning of all transformer weight matrices to a retained
density $d$. Quantization is symmetric round-to-nearest of the same
matrices, weights only, either per output channel at bit-width $b$ or
with contiguous input groups of $g$ weights sharing one scale.
Distillation is supervised fine-tuning of an existing student on
black-box teacher traces with strict LoRA adapters, where a pool of
$U$ traces per domain with $D_U$ supervised completion tokens is
processed for a fixed budget; we record the processed tokens, the
supervised completion tokens $T$, and the reuse count $E=T/D_U$. The
inputs admitted to every predictor are the parameter count $N_0$ of
the transformer weight matrices, the pretraining token count $D_0$
where disclosed, and the dense capability losses $L_{0,c}$ measured
once on the uncompressed model (the initial student for
distillation). Every prediction is written as
\begin{equation}
\widehat L_{m,c}\;=\;L^{\mathrm{obs}}_{0,c}\;+\;
\widehat\Delta_{m,c}(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta_m),\qquad
\mathbf x=(N_0,D_0,L_{0,c}),
\label{eq:interface}
\end{equation}
with $\boldsymbol\theta_m$ the setting of method $m$ (density,
bit-width and group size, or pool and budget). The function
$\widehat\Delta_{m,c}$ is method-specific; the arms share the endpoint
and the evaluation protocol, not a functional form.

\subsection{Fitting and Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Each relation is fit on development states and settings only and is
judged on two kinds of unseen target: an \emph{unseen setting} (a
density, bit-width, group size, pool, or budget outside the fitted
set) and an \emph{unseen state} (a checkpoint of a new size or
pretraining stage, or a new student). On the development panel we use
leave-one-stage-out and leave-one-source-out cross-validation; for new
states the forms, coefficients, and predictions of every candidate are
committed to the repository before the compressed outcome is
measured, and a prediction keeps its status whatever the outcome. No
prediction uses a measurement of the target compressed model; the
dense anchor $L_{0,c}$ is the only measured input.

Error is the mean absolute error of $\widehat\Delta$ in nats per
token, per capability. Every candidate is compared with three
baseline classes on exactly the same cells: source-free references
(zero change, a per-capability constant, a strength-only curve, or the
median development curve), same-input alternatives (the same
$\mathbf x$ with a different functional structure), and the no-$D_0$
ablation. Where several baselines exist the strongest per capability
is reported and that choice is marked as retrospective; bootstrap
intervals are given only for the leave-one-out panels, with source
states as the resampling unit. Freeze commits, the provenance code of
each comparison, and the fold rules are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_registration}.
