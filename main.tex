\begin{abstract}
Agentic applications rarely need frontier-scale generality: they need small,
economical models whose \emph{specific combination} of capabilities is good
enough. The standard route to such models is compressing a large source
model---distillation, pruning, quantization---yet existing scaling laws for
compression live almost entirely in pretraining-loss space and say nothing
about which capabilities survive. We develop \emph{scaling-down laws}:
predictive relationships between the basic parameters of compression (source
size $N_0$, pruning density $\dens$, bit-width $\bits$, distillation data
$\Ds$, recovery tokens $\Dr$) and a \emph{vector} of capability-conditional
losses. Our framework treats scaling-down as \emph{selective deletion} in
three bases---weight coordinates (pruning), bit-planes (quantization), and
function components (distillation)---and its central object is the source
model's \emph{capability geometry}: capability-conditional gradient and
Fisher structure at the dense checkpoint. This yields testable, pre-registered
mechanisms rather than curve fitting. Across twelve dense checkpoints from
four families (Qwen3, Gemma-3/4, Muse, OLMo-3; 270M--32B) we establish:
(i) capabilities are \emph{real regions of gradient space}---independent
benchmarks of the same skill share gradient signatures that anti-align
across skills in every model tested, and zeroing a capability's exclusive
$0.05\%$ of coordinates destroys that capability alone (a near-diagonal
causal ablation matrix); (ii) pre-cliff pruning damage obeys
$\Delta L_c = \bar g_c^{\top}\delta w + B\,m_c^{\gamma}$, where the signed
alignment term and the deleted capability mass $m_c$ are \emph{measured},
$\gamma$ is shared across capabilities within a model, and the sign of the
alignment term alone predicts on which families pruning \emph{improves}
retrieval-style capability (four families called correctly, including a
within-family sign flip); (iii) the behavioral collapse
(``cliff'')---non-perturbative in loss---is a smooth threshold crossing in
geometry: capability-region mass retention decays smoothly and the cliff
sits at a family-specific critical retention; and (iv) the quantization,
distillation, and recovery arms inherit the same structure, including a
universal bit-width cliff at $3$ bits, a confirmed coverage-deletion
prediction for distillation recipes, and a recovery law whose
unrecoverable residual---about $20\%$ of pruning damage---is the true
price of scaling-down. The framework culminates in reduced-form laws in
basic parameters only, and a \emph{method-selection map} that tells
practitioners which compression route preserves a required capability
profile at the lowest cost.
\end{abstract}
