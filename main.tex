\begin{abstract}
We systematically investigate whether information observable before
compression can predict capability-conditioned loss after pruning,
quantization, and distillation. The endpoint is a per-capability
conditional loss (math, code, multi-hop QA) measured relative to the
dense source or, for distillation, the initial student; the inputs are
the source size, its pretraining tokens, its dense capability losses,
and the intervention setting. We separate transfer across source-model
states from prediction at unseen intervention settings, evaluate each
candidate against baselines with the same information budget, and
freeze predictions before new sources are measured. On a heterogeneous
panel of 12 public models from five series, responses are strongly
capability- and series-specific: QA is the least-damaged capability in
Qwen3 and OLMo-3-32B and the most-damaged one in Gemma at mild pruning,
and 3-bit quantization collapses 12 of 14 models but not OLMo-3-32B. On
a controlled Pythia panel, the dense capability vector carries the
prediction; source-conditioned pruning predictors beat source-free
curves at seen densities and at interpolated densities on the
development sources, match them at a new in-range size, and fail at
density $0.55$ and at a source five times larger; a shared power form
adds nothing over a per-density regression. Quantization
gains appear only in the collapse regime and depend on the fitting
protocol; for distillation a reuse-count coordinate helps QA on an
unseen pool with a budget-dependent bias, and a constant is best for
math and code. The results identify conditional predictive
relationships and their tested limits, providing an empirical basis for
developing capability-conditioned scaling-down laws.
\end{abstract}
