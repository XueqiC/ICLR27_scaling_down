\begin{abstract}
Agentic applications rarely need frontier-scale generality: they need small,
economical models whose \emph{specific combination} of capabilities is good
enough. The standard route to such models is compressing a large source
model---distillation, pruning, quantization---yet existing scaling laws for
compression live almost entirely in pretraining-loss space and say nothing
about which capabilities survive. We develop \emph{scaling-down laws}:
predictive relationships between the basic parameters of compression (source
size $N_0$, pruning density $\dens$, bit-width $\bits$, distillation data
$\Ds$, recovery tokens $\Dr$) and a \emph{vector} of capability-conditional
losses. Our framework treats scaling-down as \emph{selective deletion} in
three bases---weight coordinates (pruning), bit-planes (quantization), and
function components (distillation)---and its central object is the source
model's \emph{capability geometry}: capability-conditional gradient and
Fisher structure at the dense checkpoint. This yields testable, pre-registered
mechanisms rather than curve fitting. Across eleven dense checkpoints from
four families (Qwen3, Gemma-3/4, Muse, OLMo-3; 270M--31B) we find that
scaling-down has \emph{family-invariant form with family-specific
coefficients}: (i) capability ordering under pruning is universal (multi-hop
QA most robust, math and code most fragile, in every family and size);
(ii) the \emph{signed} first-order term of capability geometry is the family
coefficient and predicts in both directions---it is negative for Qwen3's QA,
which measurably \emph{improves} while pruning up to half of all weights
(predicted $-0.83$ vs.\ measured $-0.79$ at 4B, density $0.7$), and positive
for Gemma's, which never improves; (iii) collapse is universal, confined to
a narrow proportional density band across a $115\times$ size range, and
non-perturbative---no local expansion sees it coming---making scaling-down
laws two-regime laws; and (iv) within-family robustness peaks at mid scale
in two families independently, a reproducible pattern for which we retire
our own pre-registered spectral predictor. The framework culminates in
reduced-form laws in basic parameters only, and a \emph{method-selection
map} that tells practitioners which compression route preserves a required
capability profile at the lowest cost.
\end{abstract}
