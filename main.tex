\begin{abstract}
Capability-specialized agents rarely need frontier-scale generality: a
coding or retrieval agent needs a specific \emph{capability profile} at the
lowest deployment cost. The standard route is compressing a dense source
model---pruning, quantization, or distillation, usually with recovery
training---but choosing among methods today means expensive grid search,
because compression changes capabilities anisotropically and existing
compression scaling laws predict only aggregate pretraining loss. We
develop \emph{predictive scaling-down laws}: from a dense source state
that is observable \emph{before} compression (an observed capability
anchor plus public source descriptors), common compression coordinates,
and a small number of method-specific controls, predict the
post-compression capability vector---and from it, workload performance
and the cheapest configuration meeting a requirement. Across twelve dense
checkpoints from four families (Qwen3, Gemma-3/4, Muse, OLMo-3;
270M--32B) we find: (i) capability ordering under pruning is invariant
(multi-hop QA most robust, math and code most fragile in every model),
while magnitudes and signs are family coefficients---on some families
pruning measurably \emph{improves} retrieval-style capability;
(ii) independent benchmarks of the same skill share gradient signatures
that anti-align across skills in all seven models tested, and in a first
single-model test, zeroing a capability's exclusive $0.05\%$ of
coordinates selectively destroys that capability---mechanism evidence
that capability-conditioned prediction targets real structure;
(iii) every model meets a collapse cliff in a narrow proportional density
band that local expansions do not anticipate, while a geometric retention
statistic decays smoothly and crosses a family-consistent critical value
at the cliff; and (iv) quantization shows the same selective structure
with a collapse at 3 bits in all nine models measured, distillation
recipes that strip chains of thought selectively damage math, and
recovery training follows a saturating law whose asymptotic residual
under a fixed recipe quantifies what compression cost cannot buy back.
We organize these observations into an anchored, shared-plus-%
method-specific law hierarchy with held-out validation, and into a
\emph{method-selection map} for practitioners; components still under
validation are explicitly marked throughout.
\end{abstract}
